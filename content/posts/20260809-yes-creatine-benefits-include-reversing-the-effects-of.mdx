Most guys think creatine is just for bigger muscles. Turns out your brain runs on the same fuel your muscles do, and when you're sleep-deprived or stressed to hell, that fuel tank empties fast. A new batch of research shows creatine can actually refill it.

Here's the mechanism: your brain uses creatine phosphate to generate ATP, the energy currency that keeps your neurons firing. When you're not sleeping or you're under sustained stress, your brain's creatine stores drop. Add creatine supplementation and you're essentially topping off the tank. One study found that sleep-deprived subjects given creatine performed better on cognitive tasks and showed less mental fatigue than those given a placebo. Another showed similar benefits for people under psychological stress.

The catch is dosing. You're not talking about 5 grams a day here. The research that matters uses higher doses, typically 20 grams daily split into four 5-gram doses for five to seven days to load it, then 3 to 5 grams daily to maintain. That's a bigger commitment than the standard muscle-building protocol, and frankly, most studies on cognition and stress used doses at the higher end. The good news is creatine is dirt cheap, it's been studied for decades, and side effects at these doses are minimal for most people. Water retention is real but temporary.

The hard truth: this isn't a substitute for actual sleep or stress management. But if you're in a phase of life where sleep is genuinely scarce or stress is non-negotiable, creatine appears to be one of the few supplements that actually has your brain's back when things get rough.
