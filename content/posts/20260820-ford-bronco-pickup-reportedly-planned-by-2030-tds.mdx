Ford's cooking up a Bronco pickup, and it's supposed to land sometime before 2030. That's the word from The Drive, based on what sounds like internal planning docs or industry chatter. If it actually happens, it's a smart move—the Bronco nameplate has turned into a genuine cash engine for Ford since they brought it back in 2021, and slapping that badge on a truck would let them tap into the same crowd that's already buying the Bronco SUV and the Bronco Sport. A Bronco truck sits in this weird gap where it's rugged enough to feel authentic but styled enough to appeal to guys who want personality in their off-roader, not just utility. Ford's already proven they can execute on the brand with multiple body styles, so a pickup version isn't some wild swing in the dark.

The timing matters though. 2030 is seven years away, which is long enough for the market to shift significantly. By then, electric trucks will be more established, battery costs will drop further, and consumer attitudes toward EVs will have solidified. Ford could be planning a combustion engine truck, a hybrid, or even a full battery option depending on which direction the market tilts. The Bronco brand has always been about capability and attitude, and that translates across powertrains better than it does for, say, a mainstream sedan. Whether they actually pull the trigger depends on whether the Bronco SUV stays hot and whether Ford's profit margins on trucks are still fat enough to justify the development cost. But right now, on paper, a Bronco pickup makes sense.
