The real problem with AI isn't that kids are drowning in garbage content. It's that their parents are the ones building the pipes and flooding the system with it.

Chuck Wendig nails something that gets buried under all the pearl-clutching about TikTok algorithms and Discord bots. Adults have the tools, the training data, the decision-making power, and the profit motive. Kids are just the audience getting fed whatever their parents' generation decides to optimize for. The slop isn't accidental. It's engineered. And the people engineering it aren't teenagers recording themselves on their phones. They're middle-aged engineers in California deciding that engagement metrics matter more than signal quality, that scale matters more than accuracy, that surveillance is the only business model that works.

This flips the entire conversation we're supposed to be having. We treat AI like this external threat kids need to be protected from, when the actual threat is us. Adult choices about how to build these systems, what to train them on, how to monetize them, what safeguards to skip for speed and profit. The conversation about "kids and AI" is really a conversation about whether parents are willing to demand their own generation actually build something worth defending.
