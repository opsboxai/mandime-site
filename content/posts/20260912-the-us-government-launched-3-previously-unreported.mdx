The US government's been quietly digging into Polymarket trades that stink of insider information, and the details are way more sprawling than anyone knew. According to documents WIRED obtained through FOIA requests, the Commodity Futures Trading Commission launched at least three separate investigations into suspicious activity on the prediction market platform. One zeroed in on trades placed right before Biden's pardon announcements. Another tracked bets made ahead of potential Iran military action. A third went after suspicious Google stock trades. None of this was public until now.

Here's what makes it actually matter: Polymarket has exploded as the go-to place for people betting real money on everything from elections to geopolitical events. It runs on the assumption that people trading genuine skin in the game create accurate predictions. But if someone with access to classified information or corporate secrets is just running a casino on that asymmetry, the whole thing falls apart. The CFTC's job is to stop exactly that kind of fraud. That they've been running multiple investigations simultaneously suggests this isn't a one-off problem. It's a pattern.

The Biden pardon angle is particularly loaded. Polymarket saw unusual activity in advance of several presidential pardons, which means someone likely had advance knowledge of decisions that weren't public yet. That's not prediction. That's just theft of government information dressed up in betting slips. The same logic applies to the Iran trades and whatever was happening with Google. These aren't edge cases or fringe anomalies. They're evidence that the regulatory system is struggling to keep up with how prediction markets actually work in practice, and that people with access to secrets have figured out they can turn that access into cash without a lot of friction.
