Toyota's allegedly cooking up a GR GT supercar, and if the rumor sticks, it won't just be another hardtop. The word is they're bringing back the Aero Top targa configuration, that removable roof panel setup that hasn't seriously existed since the 2000s. Not a full convertible, not a fixed roof either, but something in between that lets you feel the road without the structural compromise of a drop-top.

The Aero Top is a weirdly specific flex from Toyota's history. They used it on the original 2000 GT back in the day, then again on later iterations. It's the kind of engineering detail that makes you think someone in Akihababara actually gives a damn about how a car feels to drive rather than just hitting a checklist. A removable targa panel is a pain to store, harder to engineer, and adds complexity that most manufacturers have abandoned in favor of either sunroofs or full cabins. Choosing to resurrect it suggests whoever's leading this project sees it as part of the car's identity, not just an option box.

If Toyota's truly building a mid-engine supercar under the GR performance banner, the targa setup makes sense. Less weight penalty than a full convertible, more structural rigidity than a soft-top, and you get actual open-air driving when the moment calls for it. It's a choice that feels deliberately retro in a good way, not just nostalgia for its own sake.
