Jackson Hole just bolted a via ferrata straight up Corbet's Couloir, and yes, that's exactly as absurd as it sounds. This is the ski run that's basically the American equivalent of staring into an abyss and jumping. Steep enough to make ski patrol nervous. Narrow enough that one mistake costs you everything. And now, instead of dropping in on skis, you can clip into steel cables and climb it on foot.

The resort opened three separate via ferrata routes across the couloir, which means they've turned one of the most notorious skiable descents in the country into a vertical hiking experience. The appeal is obvious if you think about it: you get the same exposure, the same view of the Tetons, the same "what the hell was I thinking" moment, except you're moving at a pace where you can actually process your mortality. Via ferrata is bolted ladder climbing, basically. Cable, rungs, carabiners. It's less extreme than free climbing and less technical than roped mountaineering. But clipped into a cable on a 40-degree slope with nothing but air below you still reads pretty clearly on the pucker meter.

The real question is who this is for. Not beginners. Not people who get shaky on tall ladders. This is for the guy who's climbed enough that scrambling feels boring, or who skis that run in his head at night and wonders what it would feel like to touch every inch of it. It's also pure Jackson Hole logic: take something that already scares people and make it accessible to a different crowd. The route doesn't require ropes or partners, just a harness and the ability to not look down for several hundred vertical feet. The editor-in-chief of Climbing tested it and came back with their hands still shaking, which tells you something.
