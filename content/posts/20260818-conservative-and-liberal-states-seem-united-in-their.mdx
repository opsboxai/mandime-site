There's something genuinely rare happening right now: both red and blue states are moving to restrict Flock Safety's automatic license plate readers, and they're doing it for completely different reasons that somehow land in the same place.

Flock cameras are basically automated surveillance that reads every plate that rolls past and stores the data. Conservative states worry about government overreach and tracking citizens without consent. Liberal states worry about the same thing, just framed through a civil liberties and racial justice lens. The result is the same legislation popping up in places that otherwise can't agree on much of anything.

What makes this actually interesting is that it's not a partisan wedge issue masquerading as one. These aren't two sides of the same argument. They're two completely separate arguments that happen to object to the same tool. A conservative state legislator thinks Flock is a threat to individual freedom from government. A liberal one thinks it's a tool that disproportionately affects marginalized communities. Both are right. Both want it gone.

The tech industry loves when surveillance tools get framed as a left-right battle because it makes killing them harder. But when the concern cuts across that divide entirely, the tool loses its natural defenders. No politician wants to be the one arguing for mass plate tracking when their voters, regardless of politics, don't want it. That's how you actually kill a bad idea.
