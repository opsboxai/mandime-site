Peak summer at Yosemite means half a million people fighting for parking, shoulder-to-shoulder hikes, and the Valley looking less like wilderness and more like a concert venue. Mary Andino figured out the actual workarounds, and they're less about secret trails and more about timing and logistics. The real move is abandoning the idea that you need to be there during daylight hours when everyone else is. Early morning starts matter, but the bigger play is visiting on weekdays in June or September when the park is still open but the Instagram crowd hasn't arrived yet. Most people book for July or August because those are school vacation months, which means if you can swing a trip outside that window, you're already ahead of 80 percent of visitors.

The second part isn't complicated but requires actually listening to it. Skip Yosemite Valley entirely on peak days and head to the high country instead. Glacier Point, the Tioga Road corridor, and the eastern Sierra entrance see a fraction of the traffic because they require a drive and a bit more effort. Parking fills up fast in the Valley, but you can find spots at Tenaya Lake or Tuolumne Meadows well into the afternoon. The trails there are just as good. You're not compromising on the experience. You're just picking the version that doesn't involve waiting in line for a bathroom.

One practical thing Andino emphasized: arrive before 8 AM or don't bother showing up that day. The park hits capacity regularly in summer, and once it does, they close the gates. It's not a maybe. If you get turned away at 9:30 AM, you're done. The people who nail it are there at sunrise or they're waiting until September. No middle ground.
