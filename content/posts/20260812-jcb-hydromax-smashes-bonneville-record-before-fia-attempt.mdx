A British construction equipment company just clocked 368 mph in a jet-powered lawnmower. That's not hyperbole. JCB, the firm that makes the yellow diggers you see at every construction site, built the Hydromax to break the land speed record, and they did it at Bonneville Salt Flats before the FIA could even officially time it.

The machine is exactly what it sounds like: a turbine-powered tank the size of a small car with a jet engine bolted to the back. They hit 368.347 mph on their first real run, which means they've already got the speed to claim the record once the governing body sanction is finished. What's wild is the sheer ambition of it. This wasn't some boutique speed shop or a hypercar manufacturer with unlimited budget. This was a digger company deciding that yes, we're going to engineer a jet vehicle and take it to the salt flats. And they did.

The FIA's official attempt is still coming, which means they could go faster. The current record sits at 763 mph set by ThrustSSC back in 1997, so JCB isn't touching that anytime soon, but in the wheeled vehicle category they're in serious contention. What matters is that someone actually committed to this insane project and executed it. No press release posturing. No vaporware. Just a team that showed up at Bonneville with an unlikely machine and proved it works.
