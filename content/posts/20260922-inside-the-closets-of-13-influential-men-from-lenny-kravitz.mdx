A three-story man cave in one corner of the world, a stark Parisian retreat in another. That's the gap between how different men actually live, and GQ just walked through 13 of their closets to prove it.

What's interesting isn't that Lenny Kravitz or Yohji Yamamoto have expensive clothes. It's what they've kept and why. A closet under real scrutiny stops being about the pieces and starts being about the person. Some guys collect. Some guys curate ruthlessly. Some guys wear the same silhouette for thirty years because it works and they've stopped asking permission to be predictable.

The thing these 13 have in common isn't a dress code. It's intention. They picked their uniform, whatever shape it took, and committed. That's the actual lesson hiding in the photo spreads. Not "buy this," but "know why you're buying it in the first place." A closet that tells a story does that because someone spent time listening to what they actually needed instead of what the internet said they should want. Most guys never get there. These ones did.
