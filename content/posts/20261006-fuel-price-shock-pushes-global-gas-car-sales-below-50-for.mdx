Gas cars just fell below half of global auto sales for the first time ever. That's not a prediction anymore. It's the reality right now.

The shift happened faster than anyone expected. High fuel prices in 2022 and 2023 accelerated what was supposed to be a slow, decades-long transition. People doing the math at the pump realized electric and hybrid vehicles suddenly made financial sense, not just environmental sense. Dealers who spent years dismissing EVs started stocking them aggressively. Supply chains that were barely online five years ago are now ramping production at scale.

What's wild is the speed of the flip. A year ago, conventional wisdom said gas would dominate for another 15 to 20 years in most markets. Instead, the combination of fuel sticker shock, improving EV range, and new models hitting every price point turned it into a crossover moment almost overnight. It's not that ICE cars are dead tomorrow. It's that for the first time in automotive history, they're no longer the default choice for new car buyers anywhere on the planet.

The real play now isn't whether EVs win. It's which manufacturers survive the next five years. Automakers caught flat-footed on battery tech and EV platforms are burning through cash to catch up. The ones who hedged their bets are getting punished. And legacy supply chains built around pistons and gasoline are becoming worthless assets overnight. That's a harder problem than just building a better EV.
