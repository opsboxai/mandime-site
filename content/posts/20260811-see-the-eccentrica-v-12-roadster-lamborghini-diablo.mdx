Eccentrica just took a 1990 Lamborghini Diablo and did something most people would consider insane: they chopped the roof clean off, kept the V-12, and turned it into a roadster that looks like it was always meant to exist that way. The thing is, it actually works. That wedge shape, those aggressive angles, the raw aggression of the Diablo's design language doesn't soften when you remove the hardtop. If anything, it gets meaner.

The build preserves what made the original special—that iconic 5.7-liter V-12 engine, the mid-mounted aggression, the unflinching 90s brutalism—while solving what was always a problem with the Diablo: you couldn't feel the thing around you. No visibility, no connection, just intent. As a roadster, it becomes something different. Still unmistakably a Diablo. Still loud and impractical and completely indifferent to your comfort. But now you're sitting in it, breathing the same air as that engine, feeling the thing move beneath you instead of piloting a jet ski made of carbon fiber.

This is the kind of restomod that actually respects the original. It's not a softened, modernized interpretation. It's the same car with one honest modification: the parts between you and the sky disappeared. That's it. And somehow that changes everything about what the thing is.
