Toyota never killed the three-door Land Cruiser. They just stopped selling it in places that mattered to most of us, which is basically the same thing until you realize they're still pumping them out in Japan at prices that make you actually angry you can't order one stateside.

The Series 70 short-wheelbase model is what happens when Toyota decides "okay, we're building an actual work truck that also happens to be bulletproof." At roughly 160 inches long, it's compact enough to fit places a full-size Cruiser never will. The engine is familiar Toyota iron, the interior is spartan in the way that makes minimalism feel intentional instead of cheap, and the price tag starts somewhere around $28,000 before conversion costs and import headaches. That's the part that stings. A brand new Land Cruiser that can actually navigate a proper technical trail, and Americans get to watch from behind the tariff wall.

The three-door isn't some nostalgia play or limited run. It's a production vehicle that's been in continuous manufacture because there's still a world outside shopping malls that needs a truck that works. It's got the same hydraulic steering, the same unshakeable chassis, and the same "will outlive your mortgage" reputation that made the original Cruisers legends. You can delete the air conditioning, strip the interior to plywood, and it'll still be the most purposeful thing in a parking lot full of mall crawlers with custom bumpers and zero trail time.

The real kicker is that this truck exists in a market where most manufacturers have already decided compact, utilitarian work vehicles are dead. Toyota hasn't. They're just not trying to convince anyone in a three-square-foot parking space that they need one.
