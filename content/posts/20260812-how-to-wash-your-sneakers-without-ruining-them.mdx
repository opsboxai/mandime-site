Most people wash their sneakers wrong, which sounds like a small thing until you realize you're basically sandblasting leather or melting glue in a washing machine. Coleman Molnar's guide cuts through the sneaker-care mythology and lands on something actually useful: the stuff that works is boring, and chasing pristine kicks is a waste of energy.

Here's the practical divide. Mesh and canvas sneakers handle a gentle hand wash in cold water with mild soap. Leather needs a soft brush and patience, not aggression. And the washing machine, despite what your brain says, will destroy the glue holding your sole to the upper, especially on anything made in the last decade where cost-cutting means weaker adhesive. Suede and nubuck are basically pretending to be sneakers anyway—a soft brush and a protectant spray before you wear them is the only real move.

The mental shift Molnar pushes is the one that actually matters. Your sneakers are going to age. The sole will yellow, the leather will crease, the canvas will get dingy. That's not failure. That's the shoe being worn, which is the entire point. The obsession with keeping everything museum-condition is backward. A well-worn pair of white leather sneakers doesn't look worse; it looks lived in. The goal isn't restoration. It's keeping them functional and not accelerating the collapse. Wash them gently, dry them flat away from heat, and stop trying to turn back time.
