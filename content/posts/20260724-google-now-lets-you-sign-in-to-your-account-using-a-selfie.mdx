Google just added selfie video as a recovery method for when you're locked out of your account. It's exactly what it sounds like: you film yourself on your phone or computer, Google's systems scan your face, and if it matches what they have on file, you're back in.

The practical upside is obvious. You lose your phone, forget your password, can't access your backup codes—suddenly you're trapped outside your own email. Now there's another lever to pull before you're dealing with Google's support queue. It works on both mobile and desktop, which covers most scenarios where you'd actually need account recovery.

The tradeoff is the one every company pretends isn't there. Google now has video of your face as part of its identity verification system. The company says the video is processed, encrypted, and deleted after verification, but you're handing them another biometric identifier stored somewhere in their infrastructure. That's useful if it saves you during a real lockout, but it's also one more piece of you in Google's database, one more vector for abuse if those systems ever get breached, and one more reason to read their updated privacy policy on how this data actually gets handled.

For most people, it's a sensible addition to the toolkit. For people who care about minimizing their digital footprint, it's worth thinking through whether the convenience is worth the expanded biometric record. The fact that Google keeps offering new recovery methods is good security design. The fact that they're all variations on "give us more data about yourself" is worth noticing.
