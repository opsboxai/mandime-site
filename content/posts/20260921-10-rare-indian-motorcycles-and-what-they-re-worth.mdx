Indian motorcycles from the early 1900s aren't just old bikes—they're the machines that actually built American motorcycling before Harley became synonymous with the word. A pristine 1913 Indian Model F chainless single sold at auction for over $65,000, and that's not even the priciest one out there. The 1908 Indian Single, essentially the company's first production model, regularly fetches $40,000 to $75,000 depending on originality and condition. What makes these numbers stick is that Indians were genuinely faster and more reliable than their competitors when they mattered most, which means the ones that survived intact did so because someone understood what they had.

The real money is in the pre-1920 machines, particularly the V-twins and anything with genuine race history. An original 1915 Indian Powerplus V-twin in excellent condition can hit $85,000 or beyond at the right auction house. What separates a $20,000 Indian from an $80,000 Indian almost always comes down to documentation, originality of parts, and whether the engine numbers match the frame. Restorers have learned the hard way that a sympathetic restoration loses serious value fast—collectors want to see what the factory actually built, patina and all.

By the 1930s, when Indian was fighting a losing battle against Harley's marketing machine, prices drop noticeably. A well-preserved 1936 Indian Chief might run $30,000 to $50,000, still substantial but a fraction of what a 1913 would fetch. The postwar machines, 1950s and later, sit in the $8,000 to $20,000 range depending on model and condition. None of it is cheap, but then again, these aren't investments for people watching spreadsheets. Anyone spending five figures on a motorcycle from 1912 is already making a statement about what they value.
