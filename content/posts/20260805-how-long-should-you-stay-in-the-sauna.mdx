The sweet spot is between 15 and 20 minutes, though your first time in might feel shorter. That's the window where your body starts pulling real benefits without crossing into the territory where you're just sitting there cooking for vanity points.

Here's what actually happens in that timeframe: your heart rate climbs, blood vessels dilate, and your body starts flushing out sweat at a serious clip. That's not just detox theater. Heat stress triggers heat shock proteins, which do actual repair work on your cells. Your nervous system shifts into recovery mode. Twenty minutes is roughly when you hit the efficiency ceiling for a single session. Stay longer and you're adding time without meaningful gains, just additional stress on your cardiovascular system that only pays off if you're already conditioned for it.

The catch is individual tolerance matters more than a fixed number. Someone hitting the sauna for the first time should aim for 10 to 15 minutes and call it. Regular users can stretch to 20 or even 30, but the research doesn't show a linear benefit past that point. Dehydration is the real limiter. You're sweating out water and electrolytes faster than you probably think. The whole thing collapses if you walk out and don't rehydrate properly, which is where most people drop the ball.

Skip the temperature gymnastics and the competitive sitting. Hit a moderate heat, stay for 15 to 20 minutes, get out, drink water, and move on. That's the protocol that actually works.
