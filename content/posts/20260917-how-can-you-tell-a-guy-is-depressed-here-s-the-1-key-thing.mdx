The cliché version of depression looks like someone who can't get out of bed, won't shower, sits in darkness waiting for it to end. That's one flavor of it. But therapists say the thing they actually watch for in men is way different: irritability. Not sadness. Irritability.

A guy can seem fine on the surface, hold down a job, keep his apartment clean, show up to work on time. But if he's snapping at his girlfriend over nothing, if small inconveniences send him into a rage, if he's cutting people off or withdrawing from friends because everything feels exhausting and pointless, that's the signal. Depression in men often wears the mask of anger. It shows up as impatience, as a shortened fuse, as the kind of guy who used to be fun at a bar and now just seems perpetually pissed off.

This matters because it means a lot of men get misdiagnosed, or never get diagnosed at all. They don't fit the textbook depression narrative, so they assume something else is wrong. Maybe they blame their job, or their relationship, or just accept that they're an irritable person now. The damage keeps going, the irritability deepens, and nobody around them realizes what's actually happening until something breaks.

If you're noticing this in yourself or someone you know, that's worth taking seriously. Depression doesn't always announce itself. Sometimes it just slowly poisons your tolerance for life.
