Telo just quietly revised the towing capacity on its MT1 electric pickup upward, and the new numbers actually matter. The startup initially claimed 7,500 pounds of towing capability, then updated that to 14,000 pounds. For context, the Ford F-150 Lightning tops out around 14,000 pounds too, and the Chevrolet Silverado EV is rated for 8,800 pounds. That's not a minor adjustment. That's the difference between hauling a trailer with actual weight behind it versus playing it safe on lighter jobs.

The MT1 is already positioned as the accessible entry point in the EV truck space, smaller and cheaper than the Cybertruck or R1T. But towing capacity was always the shadow hanging over it. A truck that can't haul means it's really just an electric car shaped like a truck, and buyers know the difference. Double the towing spec, even if real-world performance varies, and suddenly the MT1 stops being the cute option and starts being the practical one. The payload capacity sits at around 2,500 pounds, which is solid for the class.

What makes this bump significant isn't just the number itself. It's that Telo apparently found the capability was there all along. That either means early testing was overly conservative, or they figured out that the battery and drivetrain could handle more than the initial engineers thought. Either way, updated specs suggest the truck's ready for what buyers actually want to do with a pickup, not what a spec sheet thinks they should do. For a startup trying to prove it can compete with Ford and Chevy, that's the kind of detail that converts skeptics into customers.
