Monterey Car Week auctions are about to get weird in the best way possible. RM Sotheby's, Bonhams, and the other heavy hitters have loaded their catalogs with stuff that makes you wonder if the collector car market actually learned anything, or if it's just getting richer and more specific about its obsessions.

The real story isn't the headline cars everyone knows are coming. It's that the market has fractured into these laser-focused micro-categories now. You've got people dropping serious money on obscure Japanese sports cars from the 1990s, homologation specials that were built for exactly 50 racing events in 1973, and modified American muscle that cost more to restore than it cost to send someone to college. The days of "buy the obvious Ferrari" are basically over. The money is going to whoever can dig up the strangest provenance, the rarest color combo, or the weirdest one-off that's somehow still relevant to someone's specific passion.

What matters is that this year's catalogs reflect what the serious collectors actually want right now, not what auction houses think they want. That's the tell. When you see what RM Sotheby's is putting on the block, you're seeing where real wealth is actually flowing in the car world. And if the previews say anything, it's that the game is getting narrower, deeper, and way less forgiving if you don't know what you're looking at.
