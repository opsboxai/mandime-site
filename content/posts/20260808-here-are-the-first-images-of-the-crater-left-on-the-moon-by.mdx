The Korea Aerospace Research Institute (KARI) has released the first images of the crater left on the moon by a SpaceX Falcon 9 rocket stage. The Danuri orbiter, orbiting the moon at an altitude of 62 miles, captured these images shortly after the impact. The crater, while significant, is surprisingly small compared to the vast lunar surface. 

To put it into perspective, the moon's diameter is about 2,160 miles, making this impact site a tiny blemish on the lunar landscape. The images show a crater that's dozens of feet wide and nearly 12 feet deep, a testament to the force of the impact. The collision was predicted well in advance, with astronomers detecting sodium and lithium rising from the lunar surface, confirming the rocket fragment's strike.

SpaceX's Falcon 9 rocket stage had been in an unstable orbit for over a year, eventually crashing into the moon due to the gravitational pull of Earth, the moon, and the sun. SpaceX notes that for higher-energy missions, controlled deorbiting isn't always possible, and they are working to ensure space safety. The images from KARI highlight the delicate balance between space exploration and the unintended consequences of leaving hardware in orbit.
