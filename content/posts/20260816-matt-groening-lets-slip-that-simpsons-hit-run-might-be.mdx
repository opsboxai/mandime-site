Matt Groening just accidentally confirmed what a lot of people have been waiting two decades to hear. At D23, when someone asked about bringing back The Simpsons: Hit & Run, Groening said flat out, "I think the original game is coming back in some form." Then showrunner Matt Selman immediately jumped in with "or not," and the whole room got that feeling you get when someone says something they probably shouldn't have said out loud yet.

Hit & Run came out in 2003 and became the closest thing The Simpsons ever had to a legitimate video game. It was a Grand Theft Auto clone built around Springfield, and it actually worked. You drove around as Homer, Bart, Marge, and the rest doing story missions, collecting cars, causing the exact amount of chaos that made sense for the property. It wasn't trying to be GTA. It was trying to be The Simpsons in a game, which is a weirdly rare thing for licensed properties to nail. The game got delisted and basically disappeared, which meant anyone who wanted to play it in 2024 was out of luck legally.

The hedging at D23, the "or not" that came right after Groening's comment, is the tell here. Someone knows something. Whether that's a remaster, a re-release, or something built from the ground up using the original as a template, the fact that these two were having this conversation in front of cameras suggests there's something real being discussed behind closed doors. For anyone who remembers burning hours in Springfield, that's worth paying attention to.
