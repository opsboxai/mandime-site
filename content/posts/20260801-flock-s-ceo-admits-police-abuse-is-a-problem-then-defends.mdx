Flock Safety's CEO Garrett Langley just did what every corporate guy does when caught in the crosshairs: he acknowledged the problem while carefully avoiding any commitment to fix it. According to The Drive, Langley told Joel Feder that "any level of abuse is unacceptable" when asked about cops using Flock's license plate recognition cameras to stalk, harass, or target people without legitimate cause. Solid quote. Then he pivoted.

Here's the thing. Flock sells AI-powered surveillance cameras to police departments and private property owners across the country. The company has exploded in adoption because the tech works—it reads plates in real time, runs them against databases, flags hits. It's a legitimate crime-fighting tool. But it's also a perfect weapon for abuse. A cop with a vendetta, a partner trying to track an ex, someone with access looking to prove a point—all they need is one flagged plate in the system and they've got probable cause to initiate a stop. Or at minimum, they've got documentation of where someone went. The Drive has reported on exactly this happening. Multiple times.

Langley's statement amounts to a shrug dressed up as accountability. "Any level of abuse is unacceptable" is not a policy. It's not a safeguard. It's not even a promise—it's a value statement you'd expect from a company's ethics page, the kind of thing that sounds good in court if this blows up later. What actually matters is what comes next: internal auditing systems that flag suspicious access patterns, mandatory consent frameworks, transparency reports, real consequences for abuse. Langley didn't detail any of that. He defended his company. Which tells you everything about where Flock's priorities actually sit.
