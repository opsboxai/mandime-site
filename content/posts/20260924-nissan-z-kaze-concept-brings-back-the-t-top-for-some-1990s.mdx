Nissan just showed up to the concept car game with a Z Kaze that strips the roof off and bolts on a pair of removable targa panels. It's basically what the 300ZX should have been if it cared less about being a technological statement and more about feeling like wind in your hair on a Friday night.

The whole thing trades the original Z's simplicity for that specific flavor of 1990s excess that somehow worked. Dual removable panels, a wider stance, aggressive body work that doesn't look like it's apologizing for existing. This isn't a futuristic vision of what cars could be. It's nostalgia dressed up in carbon fiber, and it's honest about that. Concept cars usually try to hint at some direction the brand's going. This one just admits it misses the era when sports cars could be about pure driving experience instead of infotainment systems and autonomous features.

The T-top setup matters because it splits the difference between a convertible and a coupe in a way that disappears every five years then comes back as a "radical" idea. It's not fully exposed like a drop-top, not fully enclosed like a hardtop. That middle ground is where a specific kind of car guy lives, and Nissan clearly knows exactly who they're talking to with this one.
