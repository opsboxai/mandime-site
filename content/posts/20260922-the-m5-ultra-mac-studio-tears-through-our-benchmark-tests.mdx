Apple just released a Mac Studio with 36 CPU cores and an 80-core GPU for $12,299. That's not hyperbole about the specs, and it's not a typo on the price. The M5 Ultra is built for one thing: running massive AI workloads and professional video rendering jobs that would cripple anything else on the market.

The gap between what this machine does and what most people actually need is enormous. A base MacBook Pro handles 95 percent of creative work just fine. But if you're training models, processing 8K footage, or running the kind of compute jobs that require serious parallelization, the raw horsepower here is real. The 256GB of unified memory is the real story. Instead of shuffling data between CPU and GPU like traditional systems do, everything sits in the same pool, which means less bottleneck and faster throughput on tasks that would absolutely tank a lesser machine.

The price scales with what you're getting, but make no mistake: this is a specialist tool. It's what you buy when renting cloud time becomes more expensive than owning the hardware outright. For most people, it's theater. For the people who actually need it, it's probably the cheapest option they have.
