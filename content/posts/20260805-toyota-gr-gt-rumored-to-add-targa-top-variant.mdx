Toyota's been teasing the GR GT for months, but the actual reveal has been vaporware so far. Now Road & Track picked up fresh rumors that the production version might come with a Targa top option, which would be a genuinely smart move for a car that's supposed to be Toyota's answer to the Porsche 911.

The Targa layout solves a real problem. You get an open-air driving experience without the structural compromises of a full convertible. The roof panel comes off, you get better visibility, and the car doesn't turn into a noodle when you're pushing it. It's the reason Porsche's been selling 911 Targas for decades. If Toyota's serious about this being a driver's car, not just a straight-line monster, that's exactly the call they should make.

The other rumor floating around is that a higher-output version is coming too. Given that the base GR GT is already supposed to pack serious power from whatever turbo setup Toyota settles on, the hotter variant could be genuinely competitive against the 911 Turbo crowd. The timing would make sense too. Toyota's finally got the cash and the engineering will to build something that's actually fast, and people are hungry for Japanese sports cars that don't play it safe.

None of this is confirmed yet. But if the Targa rumor sticks, it signals that Toyota's thinking about the right things: feel over spec sheet, options over one-size-fits-all, and the kind of details that separate a car you want to own from a car that just looks good in marketing photos.
