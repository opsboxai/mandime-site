A 1966 Ford GT that spent nearly two hours in overtime bidding at auction just sold for 2.1 million dollars. The original window sticker from 1966 listed the MSRP at $174,595—which sounds wild until you adjust for inflation and realize that's still just $290,000 in today's money. So someone paid seven times what this car cost new, and they had to fight for it.

This is what happens when you combine low mileage, factory provenance, and the specific cultural weight of the Ford GT nameplate. Eight miles. The car has been driven eight miles since it left the dealership in 1966. That's not a car that was tucked away for fifty years and rediscovered—that's a car that was essentially preserved from day one, which means the mechanical and cosmetic integrity is approaching museum standard. No restorations, no amateur work, no mystery about what actually went wrong under the dash in 1987. It's original down to the details people usually skip.

The overtime bidding is the real tell. When an auction goes extended rounds like that, it means multiple serious bidders believe the reserve is fair and the car is real. It's not one collector flexing. It's competition, and that's what drives prices into stratosphere territory. Ford only built 1,664 GT40s total. This one came out of the factory with the engine and configuration that mattered. After two hours of back and forth, someone decided $2.1 million was the number. For a car that cost less than a loaded truck when it was new.
