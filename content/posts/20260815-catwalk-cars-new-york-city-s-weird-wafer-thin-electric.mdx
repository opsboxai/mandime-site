New York City's tunnel police had a vehicle so narrow it could fit through pedestrian walkways. These were the Catwalk Cars, custom-built electric three-wheelers that spent decades patrolling the Holland, Lincoln, and Queens-Midtown tunnels until they were retired around 2015. They were purpose-built for a job almost nobody remembers now: monitoring the tight, confined spaces where regular patrol cars couldn't go.

The logic was simple and weird in equal measure. Tunnels need constant inspection for structural damage, leaks, debris, and safety hazards. You can't send a full-size cruiser down a walkway. So the city commissioned these miniature electric vehicles, narrow enough to slide through emergency passages but equipped with all the basics: lights, radio, two-way communication. They looked like golf carts that had been put through a compressor. Officers drove them at low speeds through the guts of New York's underground infrastructure, the kind of shift that barely existed in the public imagination.

What makes this genuinely strange is how long they lasted and how quietly they disappeared. These things were rolling around Manhattan for decades, doing actual police work in spaces most New Yorkers never knew existed. Then, without fanfare, they got phased out. No viral moment, no nostalgia wave, no Instagram thread. Just an obsolete chapter of city history that Jalopnik managed to excavate. It's a reminder that not every interesting vehicle makes it into the cultural conversation. Sometimes the weirdest transportation stories are the ones hiding in tunnels, literally.
