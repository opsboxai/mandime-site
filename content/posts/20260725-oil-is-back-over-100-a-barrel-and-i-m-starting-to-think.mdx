Oil just broke back over $100 a barrel, and yeah, it's exactly as annoying as it sounds. Brent crude hit that threshold this week, which means your next fill-up is about to hurt more than it already does. The thing that gets under your skin isn't just the number itself. It's the feeling that we've been here before, watched prices spike like this during every geopolitical hiccup for the past fifteen years, and nobody in the actual supply chain seems particularly motivated to solve it.

The war in Ukraine is still grinding on. Middle East tensions are simmering. And somehow that translates directly into oil producers deciding this is a great time to tighten supply and watch the price climb. It's the oldest play in the book: create scarcity, keep the profit margins absurd, and let everyone else eat the cost. Refineries aren't overwhelmed. There's no actual shortage. It's just deliberate. The people who control these wells know exactly what they're doing, and they're banking on the fact that you need fuel more than you need financial breathing room.

What makes it worse is the inevitability of it. Prices spike, politicians talk tough for a week, nothing structural changes, and then we all just accept the new baseline as normal. By next year, a hundred-dollar barrel will feel quaint. Meanwhile, gas prices stay elevated, logistics costs go up, everything that gets shipped gets more expensive, and that trickles down to literally everything you buy. It's not complicated. It's just frustrating as hell, because the system is designed so that the people actually pulling the levers have zero incentive to pull them differently.
