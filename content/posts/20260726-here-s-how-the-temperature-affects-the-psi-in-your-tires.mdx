For every 10 degrees Fahrenheit your tire temperature drops, you lose about 1 PSI. That's not a guess or a rough estimate. That's physics, and it hits hardest in winter, which is why your low-pressure warning light suddenly goes haywire when November hits.

Most guys know underinflated tires are bad. What they don't realize is that a 30-degree temperature swing from fall to winter can cost you 3 full PSI without you touching anything. Your tires were fine in September at 32 PSI. By January, they're at 29 PSI, and your fuel economy starts tanking along with your tire life. Under-inflated rubber doesn't roll as efficiently, burns more gas, and generates heat faster, which accelerates tread wear and aging. You're spending more money at the pump and replacing tires sooner for a problem you didn't even notice.

The fix is stupidly simple but almost nobody does it consistently. Check your tire pressure monthly, and adjust it seasonally. Don't use the max PSI printed on your tire sidewall, that's the absolute ceiling. Use the number on the driver's door jamb or your manual, that's your actual target. In summer, you might add a PSI or two. In winter, expect to lose them. Forty seconds with a gauge and compressor every few months saves you hundreds in wasted fuel and premature wear over a year. It's the easiest maintenance item most people completely ignore until something fails.
