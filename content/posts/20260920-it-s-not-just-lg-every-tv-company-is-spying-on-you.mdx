Gamers Nexus just dropped a two-hour investigation proving what you probably already suspected but didn't want to confirm: your TV is actively working against you. Not just LG's models. Every major manufacturer is doing it. The cameras are always listening, the microphones never really turn off, and your viewing habits are getting packaged and sold to the highest bidder before you finish your first episode.

Here's what makes this different from the usual privacy paranoia. Gamers Nexus didn't speculate or scare monger. They documented specific vulnerabilities, showed how the data collection works in real time, and proved that even when your TV claims to be powered down, it's still recording audio and transmitting it. Samsung, Roku, Amazon Fire, TCL, Sony. The list is long enough that it stops mattering which brand you own. If you bought it in the last five years, assume it's compromised.

The kicker is that most of this happens because manufacturers embedded microphones and cameras for voice controls and motion detection, then either didn't bother securing them properly or actively chose not to because it was cheaper. Privacy features exist. They just aren't enabled by default because there's more money in the alternative. Your viewing data is worth something to advertisers. Your location data derived from what you watch is worth something to data brokers. Your audio recordings, even the ones that seem to be deleted, might be worth something to someone.

The real problem isn't that your TV spies on you. It's that you accepted the terms of service without reading them, the manufacturer knows you didn't read them, and by the time you realize what's happening, the data is already gone and sold. Gamers Nexus showed the technical proof. The question now is whether anyone actually does anything about it, or if this just becomes another outrage that fades in a week while you keep using the same TV.
