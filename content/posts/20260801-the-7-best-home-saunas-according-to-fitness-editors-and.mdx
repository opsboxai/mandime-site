Home sauna companies are betting you'll eventually want to sweat in your own space instead of waiting for a gym slot or spa day. The smart move is knowing which ones actually hold up versus which ones are just Instagram-friendly boxes that'll collect dust.

Almost Heaven makes cedar saunas that actually last because the wood does the work. Cedar naturally resists rot and temperature swings, so you're not fighting the material. Sun Home went the prefab route with their modular kits, which means faster setup and less "why is there a gap here" regret. Higherdose leans infrared, which heats you directly instead of heating the air around you, so you get warm faster and use less energy doing it.

The real choice comes down to what you're actually committing to. Traditional saunas need proper ventilation and space, so if you're thinking corner of the garage, you need to plan for that. Infrared units are more forgiving about placement. Cedar costs more upfront but doesn't need constant maintenance. Prefab saves time but sometimes feels lighter than the real thing. None of this matters if you buy it and never use it, so pick the one that fits your actual routine, not the one that looks best in a photo.
