Ram is recalling 1.27 million first and second-generation 1500 pickups because the seat belt anchors in the second row weren't properly attached to the frame. That's a massive number, and it hits a real problem: a loose anchor point means the belt won't do its job in a crash, which defeats the entire point of having it there.

The issue stems from a manufacturing defect where the welds or fasteners holding those anchors didn't meet spec. It's not like the belts themselves are bad. It's the attachment points. In a collision, especially a side impact or rollover, that loose anchor could fail completely, leaving rear passengers unrestrained. Ram is aware of at least some complaints, though there's no word yet on whether there have been injuries.

For anyone who owns one of these trucks, this is a straight-up get-to-the-dealer situation. The fix is straightforward: they'll reinforce or replace the anchor points. It's free, it takes a couple hours probably, and there's no real reason to delay it. This isn't one of those recalls you can ignore. Seat belt systems are basic safety, and when they fail, they fail hard.
