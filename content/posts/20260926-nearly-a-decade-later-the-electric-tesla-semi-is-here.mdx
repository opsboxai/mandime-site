Tesla finally delivered the first production Semi trucks to PepsiCo in Nevada this week, and it matters more than it sounds. This isn't a flashy product launch for consumers. It's a delivery to a beverage company's logistics operation in the desert. But that's exactly the point: the electric Semi just crossed from vaporware into actual work.

The truck was announced almost a decade ago. Since then, electric semis have become the obvious next frontier for both Tesla and traditional automakers scrambling to prove they take decarbonization seriously. But announcements are cheap. Getting a 80,000-pound vehicle to haul cargo across real routes at scale is the thing nobody's actually done yet. PepsiCo buying in, at whatever the cost premium is, signals that fleet operators think the economics might finally work. Not eventually. Now.

The real test starts immediately. A Semi has to operate profitably against diesel trucks, cover its higher upfront cost through fuel savings and maintenance cuts, and hold a charge across the unpredictable demands of an actual supply chain. PepsiCo's fleet will run short regional routes from its Nevada facility, which is the smart play for an early customer. But it's also the move that proves electric trucks aren't a publicity stunt. They're becoming the alternative.
