If you're thinking about relocating, fitness infrastructure matters less than you'd think. Kathleen Rellihan at Outside just mapped out the country's 10 fittest cities, and the real story isn't the gyms or the CrossFit boxes—it's what's free and waiting outside your door.

The distinction matters because a city full of runners and cyclists isn't built that way by accident. Places that consistently rank high in fitness metrics tend to have something in common: topography, weather, and trail access that makes moving outdoors feel natural instead of like a chore. A city with a legitimate river path system or mountains thirty minutes out will shift how you train. You'll gravitate toward it. Same thing happens with bike infrastructure or flat terrain that makes commuting on two wheels actually viable. That's not motivation. That's design.

The Outside ranking pulls from real data—walkability scores, fitness participation rates, access to outdoor recreation—but the useful part is seeing which cities have built environments where staying active isn't a lifestyle choice you have to commit to, it's just what people do. Some of these places are obvious candidates. Others will surprise you. The takeaway: if you're serious about staying fit long-term, location shapes behavior more than discipline does. Moving to a city built for movement doesn't guarantee anything, but it stacks the deck. Check where they ranked yours, and if it's lower than you'd expect, that might be worth thinking about the next time you're considering a change.
