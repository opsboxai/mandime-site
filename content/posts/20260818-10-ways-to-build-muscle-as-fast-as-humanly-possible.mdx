The thing that separates guys who actually get bigger from guys who just show up to the gym is consistency paired with one specific detail: progressive overload. That's not sexy, and it won't fit on a listicle, but it's the foundation everything else sits on. Dean Stattmann pulled together actual experts for this one, and they all circle back to the same principle: you have to ask your muscles to do more work than they did last time, whether that's adding weight, reps, or volume. Without that signal, your body has zero reason to build tissue.

The rest of it matters, but it's secondary. Protein intake, sleep, recovery timing, exercise selection, tempo, frequency, range of motion—all of it moves the needle, but only if that primary lever is being pulled first. Most guys skip steps. They nail the gym work but sabotage it with trash sleep or protein that doesn't add up. Others dial in nutrition perfectly and then waste it on random exercises in random orders. The cheat sheet angle here is real because the shortcut is just doing the fundamentals without the noise. Pick compound movements, hit them hard with intention, eat enough to recover, sleep like your gains depend on it—because they do. Everything else is optimization. And optimization only works when the base is already locked in.
