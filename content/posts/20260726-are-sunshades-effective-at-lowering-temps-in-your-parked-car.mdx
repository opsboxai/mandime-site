The number that matters: a quality sunshade cuts interior temps by 10 to 20 degrees Fahrenheit on a hot day. That's real, measurable protection, and it's the difference between a steering wheel you can touch and one that'll blister your palm.

But here's where it gets interesting. Jalopnik dug into the actual data, and the results depend hard on what you're using. Cheap reflective shades do the job, sure, but they're fighting uphill against the laws of physics. A dark car in full sun can hit 150 degrees inside in under an hour. A shade buys you some of that back, but it's not a cure. The shade's only working on the windshield. The roof, side windows, and dash are still cooking.

The real winner? Cracking your windows an inch or two combined with a shade. It's so simple it's almost stupid, but it works. Air circulation breaks the seal that turns your car into an oven. Some guys go further and use those magnetic or adhesive shades for the side windows, which helps, but you're dealing with diminishing returns and a lot of hassle taking them on and off.

The takeaway: a sunshade is cheap insurance and absolutely worth the five dollars. It'll keep your car noticeably cooler and save some wear on your interior. Just don't expect it to be a standalone solution on a brutal day. Pair it with cracking a window, park in shade when you can, and you've got a real strategy.
