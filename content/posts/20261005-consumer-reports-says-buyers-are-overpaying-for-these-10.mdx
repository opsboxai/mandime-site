Consumer Reports just flagged ten new cars that buyers are regularly overpaying for, and the gap between what people actually pay and what these things are worth is wide enough to matter. Some of these cars are selling above MSRP in the current market, which means dealers are charging extra on top of the already-inflated list price. That's a markup on a markup, and it's happening across brands that span from mainstream to luxury.

The real story here is dealer markup. Sticker price is almost fictional at this point. The car shows up at the lot, the salesman sees demand, and suddenly there's a $5,000 or $10,000 "market adjustment" slapped on before you even walk in. Consumer Reports tracked which models this happens to most consistently, and if your name is on that list, you're basically a target. The data matters because it tells you which cars to either negotiate harder on or walk away from entirely.

The practical move is simple. If you're shopping one of these ten, know the real market value before you step on the lot. Use that data to push back on whatever number they put on the window. Some dealers will negotiate. Others won't. Find one that will, or buy a different car. There are cars out there that aren't being flipped the second they roll off the truck. This list is a heads-up on which ones to be skeptical about.
