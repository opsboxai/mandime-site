A used 911 that cost $280,000 five years ago will run you $380,000 today. That's not inflation or market creep. That's a three-year-old sports car appreciating like a hedge fund.

The study tracking Porsche 911 pricing found that 2016-2019 model year cars have climbed nearly $100,000 in resale value since 2019. For context, that's faster than most people's salaries grow in a lifetime. A base 911 Carrera from that era has gone from roughly $280,000 used to $380,000. Higher trims and special editions have swung even harder.

The obvious culprits are supply chain chaos, production delays on new 911s, and the simple fact that rich people stopped being patient. When you can't get a new car in under two years, the used market becomes the only option. And when it's the only option, prices find their level based on who wants it most, not what it cost new. A lightly driven 2018 911 sitting on a dealer lot in 2022 represented actual availability in a market strangled for inventory.

But here's the sharper angle: these older 911s are holding value because they're still objectively good cars. A 2016 911 Carrera is faster, more reliable, and more fun than 95 percent of what people actually own. It just didn't age like a depreciating appliance the way used luxury cars usually do. If you bought one in 2019 thinking it was a reasonable secondhand sports car, you accidentally made a smart investment. If you're shopping now, you're not buying a car. You're bidding against every other person in the world who wants a 911 and can't get a new one.
