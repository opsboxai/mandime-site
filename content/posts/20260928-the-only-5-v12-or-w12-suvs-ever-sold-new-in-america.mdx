There have been exactly five V12 or W12 SUVs sold brand-new in America, and that's it. Five. In the entire history of the market. That fact alone tells you something about the absurdity of strapping twelve cylinders into a machine designed to haul families to soccer practice.

The list is basically a museum of automotive excess. You've got the Lamborghini LM002, which arrived in 1988 and was genuinely the first of its kind in the US. Then the Aston Martin DBX, which finally brought twelve-cylinder luxury SUV engineering into the 2020s. In between and alongside these were the BMW X7 and X5 with their massive twin-turbo V12s, the Range Rover with its supercharged V12, and the Rolls-Royce Cullinan, because of course there's a twelve-cylinder Rolls-Royce SUV. Each one was built for people who didn't just want a fast SUV, they wanted the fastest, most excessive SUV imaginable. And they were willing to pay six figures, sometimes seven, to get it.

What's wild is how recent most of this is. The LM002 came and went in the eighties and nineties. Then nothing for decades. No one wanted to engineer a V12 SUV until luxury automakers realized their customers genuinely would buy anything with enough badges and enough power. Now you can get a Rolls-Royce Cullinan that costs more than some houses and happens to have a V12 under the hood. The market for twelve-cylinder SUVs isn't about practicality or even performance. It's about flex, pure and simple. And in America, there's always been room for exactly that kind of nonsense.
