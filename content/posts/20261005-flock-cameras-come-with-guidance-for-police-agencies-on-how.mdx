Flock Safety isn't just selling police departments surveillance cameras. They're also selling them a playbook on how to control the conversation around it.

Internal documents obtained by Jalopnik show that Flock packages PR guidance alongside their license plate recognition hardware, complete with talking points for city councils, media responses, and strategies to preempt privacy concerns before they gain traction. The company provides police agencies with ready-made messaging that frames the cameras as crime-fighting tools while sidestepping harder questions about data retention, who accesses the footage, and how often the system produces false matches. It's a full-service operation: hardware plus narrative management.

This matters because it's essentially turning public safety infrastructure into a branded product with built-in spin. When a police chief defends Flock cameras at a town hall using language that came from Flock's own strategic playbook, citizens aren't hearing independent analysis of whether these systems actually work or what the real privacy tradeoffs are. They're hearing a sales pitch dressed up as law enforcement policy. The documents reveal an uncomfortable truth: Flock has a vested interest in normalizing mass surveillance, and they've figured out that controlling what gets said about it is as important as the technology itself.
