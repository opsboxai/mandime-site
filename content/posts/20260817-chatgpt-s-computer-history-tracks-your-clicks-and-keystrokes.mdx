OpenAI just shipped a feature that watches everything you do on your Mac, and yeah, it's exactly as creepy as that sounds. Computer History, now live in ChatGPT's desktop app, logs your clicks, keystrokes, window switches, and screenshots to build what OpenAI calls a "timeline" of your work. The stated goal is solid in theory: train the AI to understand your workflows, suggest automations, and pick up tasks you abandoned mid-afternoon. In practice, you're handing the company a continuous video feed of your digital life, packaged and indexed for reference whenever you chat with the bot.

The catch is the usual one. You can toggle it off, sure, but it's on by default. OpenAI says the data stays on your machine and doesn't train the broader model, which is technically reassuring until you remember that "on your machine" still means accessible to OpenAI when you use the app, and "doesn't train the model" is a promise worth exactly as much as the last time a tech company made one. They're also vague about retention, compliance, and what happens when you sync accounts or upgrade devices. The feature works only on macOS for now, but assume Windows is coming.

The real issue isn't the technology itself. It's the asymmetry. OpenAI gets incredibly granular data about how you actually work, what you struggle with, what you automate, what you abandon. You get AI suggestions and a faster way to ask questions you could already ask. That's not a partnership. That's surveillance with a convenience tax. If you care about what's flowing out of your machine, dig into the settings. If you don't, just know what you're trading and make peace with it.
