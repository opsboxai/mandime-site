The thing most guys get wrong about dieting is thinking there's one right answer. Men's Health went to actual registered dietitians and came back with six completely different approaches, each one built for a different goal. That alone should tell you something: your buddy's keto success doesn't mean keto is your move.

The real insight here is that the best diet is the one you'll actually stick to. A dietitian can tell you Mediterranean eating works for heart health and longevity. Another will walk you through calorie counting if fat loss is the only metric that matters. A third might map out a high-protein structure if you're chasing muscle. What they won't do, if they're worth listening to, is sell you on one gospel. The differences between these approaches matter less than picking one and not bailing after two weeks because you're tired of the food.

The smarter move is matching the diet to your actual life. If you travel constantly, carnivore or simple calorie counting beats any plan with meal-prep complexity. If you cook at home and like variety, Mediterranean or balanced macro approaches slot in easier. If you're training hard four times a week, protein-forward eating with decent carbs beats restriction. The dietitians know this. That's why they handed over options instead of a sermon.
