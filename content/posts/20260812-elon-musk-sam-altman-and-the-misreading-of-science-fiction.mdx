Silicon Valley read the same books as everyone else, but somehow came away with completely different conclusions. Jill Lepore digs into how tech's biggest names have systematically misinterpreted the sci-fi classics they claim inspire them. Elon Musk, Sam Altman, and their peers have taken stories explicitly about the dangers of unchecked power, technological arrogance, and systems spiraling beyond human control, then used them as blueprints for exactly that kind of behavior.

The Foundation series warned about the collapse of civilization when you concentrate knowledge and decision-making in too few hands. The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy mocked corporate logic and bureaucratic absurdity. Asimov and Adams weren't writing instruction manuals. They were writing cautionary tales. But reading a warning as inspiration requires a very specific kind of blindness, the kind that comes from believing you're the hero of the story rather than the cautionary example in it.

This matters because these aren't guys casually misremembering plots. They're using these narratives to justify real decisions about real systems. When you genuinely believe you're the rational actor in a Foundation story, you stop listening to people who might point out what you're actually doing. The sci-fi gets weaponized into philosophy gets weaponized into engineering. The books become permission structures instead of warnings, which is exactly backwards from what their authors intended.
