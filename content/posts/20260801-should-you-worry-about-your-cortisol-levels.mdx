The internet's latest health obsession is cortisol, the stress hormone that TikTok has convinced half the population is secretly destroying their skin, sleep, and jawline. The thing is, your body actually needs cortisol. It's not some optional add-on that evolution threw in by mistake. The real problem is when your cortisol stays elevated for weeks or months straight, which does genuinely mess with your immune system, metabolism, and recovery. But here's where the discourse breaks down: most people talking about their "cortisol glow-down" are actually describing a bad week at work, not a chronic endocrine disorder.

The actual science is less dramatic. Cortisol peaks in the morning to wake you up, drops through the day, and hits bottom at night so you can sleep. That's healthy. What matters is the rhythm staying intact, not the absolute number. GQ dug into what actual endocrinologists say happens when cortisol goes haywire: you get stubborn weight gain that doesn't respond to diet, your sleep becomes inconsistent and shallow, your skin barrier tanks, and your mood tanks with it. Those symptoms are real. They're also not usually fixed by a $40 adaptogenic supplement or a yoga app. They require actual diagnosis, often from a doctor who actually measures your cortisol across a full day, not Instagram speculation.

The useful part of the cortisol conversation is what it got right by accident. Chronic stress does flatten your results, sleep does matter more than most guys think it does, and your morning routine sets your hormone baseline for the entire day. That's solid. The part that's overblown is the idea that you're one breath work session away from fixing a problem that might need real intervention. If you're sleeping well, exercising consistently, and not spiraling about work every night, your cortisol is probably fine. If you're not, a blood test beats a TikTok diagnosis every time.
