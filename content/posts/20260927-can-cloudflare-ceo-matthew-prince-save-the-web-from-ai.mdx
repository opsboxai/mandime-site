The Verge sat down with Cloudflare's Matthew Prince, and the conversation landed on something most tech CEOs avoid: the internet actually has a real problem, and it's not one that can be solved by throwing more AI at it. Prince runs the infrastructure that sits between roughly 20 percent of all web traffic and the internet at large, which means he watches the same patterns everyone else misses until they're already broken.

Two and a half years ago, when Prince last appeared on the show, the pivot points felt seismic. Now they feel quaint. The question isn't whether AI will reshape the web anymore. It's whether the web survives the reshaping intact. That's the tension Prince works in: Cloudflare's entire business depends on the internet remaining open and functional, which puts him at odds with every major tech company trying to close it off or monetize it in ways that break the underlying architecture.

The interview gets at something most tech coverage misses. It's not actually about whether AI is good or bad. It's about incentives. Google benefits from a certain kind of web. OpenAI benefits from a different kind. Advertisers want yet another. And somewhere in that pile of competing interests, the thing that actually made the web valuable in the first place gets incrementally worse for everyone except the people extracting value from it. Prince's position is weird because Cloudflare doesn't win by breaking things. They win when the internet works. That's a rare alignment in tech right now, and it might be the only reason someone's actually asking these questions out loud.
