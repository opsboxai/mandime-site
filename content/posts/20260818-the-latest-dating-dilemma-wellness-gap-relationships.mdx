The fundamental problem with modern dating isn't that people want different things. It's that those different things are now *visible*, quantified, and broadcast constantly. One person is tracking macros and sunrise workouts on their Apple Watch. The other is sleeping until noon and ordering delivery. Twenty years ago, you could just not talk about it. Now one partner watches the other's activity ring close at midnight while they're three glasses deep into a Wednesday night.

Rebecca Dolan calls this the wellness gap, and it's a real fracture line in how couples operate. It's not about being fit versus being lazy. It's about living in completely different value systems that touch everything: how you spend money, time, free weekends, what you do with your body, when you sleep, what you eat, how you talk about yourself. One person sees fitness as identity and discipline. The other sees it as optional. Neither is wrong, but they're speaking different languages about what a good life actually looks like.

The trap is that wellness culture has made the gap *impossible to hide*. There's no neutral ground anymore. You're either optimizing or you're not. You're either conscious of what you put in your body or you're not. The judgement is baked in. And dating someone across that divide means constantly negotiating whose version of "health" wins, or whether you're both just slowly resenting each other for not picking the same lane. The real problem isn't the gap itself. It's that we've turned wellness into a moral statement instead of just a preference, and now it's bleeding into every relationship decision we make.
