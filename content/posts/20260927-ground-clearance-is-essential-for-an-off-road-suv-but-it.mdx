Ground clearance gets all the attention when guys start shopping for an off-road rig, and yeah, it matters. But obsessing over that one number is like thinking a bigger engine makes you a better driver. You can have 12 inches of clearance and still get hung up on a rock ledge because your approach angle is garbage, or because your wheelbase is longer than a parking spot and you can't climb out of a ravine without scraping the belly.

The real off-road game is built on geometry. Approach angle, departure angle, ramp breakover angle, wheelbase, track width. Ground clearance is just one piece of a puzzle that most manufacturers deliberately make hard to find in the spec sheet. A Jeep Wrangler with 10.8 inches of ground clearance will eat lunch off a rock crawler with 11.5 inches if the Jeep's got the right angles and a shorter wheelbase. The crawler might be taller, but it can't use that height if the frame drags before the wheels do.

Then there's how you actually drive the thing. Ground clearance matters most on consistent terrain, but on technical trails you're managing angles, weight transfer, and line choice. Pick the wrong line on a boulder field and six inches of extra ground clearance won't save you. Pick the right line and you don't need as much as you think. That's why experienced off-roaders can take older, lower vehicles deeper into the backcountry than weekend warriors in the latest jacked-up truck. They know what they're doing with the fundamentals.

If you're shopping for an off-road SUV, don't get suckered by the ground clearance flex. Get the full geometry spec. Look at the angles. Check the wheelbase. Then think about where you're actually driving and whether you're chasing specs or chasing competence.
