August 2026 is going to be stupid good for anyone who actually looks up. The Perseid meteor shower peaks around August 11 and 12, and we're talking 50 to 100 meteors per hour if you're away from light pollution. That's not rare or marginal. That's a genuinely dense show that happens once a year, and it's one of the most reliable on the calendar.

But here's what makes August actually wild: there's also an annular solar eclipse on August 12. You read that right. The same month gives you one of the best meteor showers and a chance to watch the moon slip in front of the sun with that trademark ring of fire still visible around the edges. Not every year stacks like this. Most years you're picking between one good event or another. This isn't that.

The window for the eclipse is narrow and geography-dependent. If you're in Greenland, northern Canada, or parts of Spain and Portugal, you've got a direct shot. Everywhere else gets a partial eclipse, which is fine but doesn't hit the same. Plan ahead if you're serious about it. The meteor show, though, plays almost everywhere. Get out of the city, let your eyes adjust for 20 minutes, and just watch the sky. August 12 is the peak, but the Perseids run from about August 9 through 13, so you've got a window.
