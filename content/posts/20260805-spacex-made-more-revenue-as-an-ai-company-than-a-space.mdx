SpaceX just made more money selling compute to AI companies than it did launching rockets. That's the actual story buried in their latest numbers: the AI division hit $2.6 billion in revenue, more than triple the year before, while the space business did its thing in the background. For a company literally famous for sending rockets to orbit, that's a seismic shift.

Here's what happened. SpaceX started renting out its computational infrastructure to other AI outfits. Turns out there's a massive appetite for processing power from companies training large language models and running inference at scale, and SpaceX had idle capacity sitting around. They filled it. The margins are cleaner than rocket launches, the revenue is more predictable, and unlike orbital mechanics, you don't have to nail a landing to make money. So they scaled it hard.

The space business didn't shrivel. Starlink grew. Government contracts stayed steady. But the relative economics flipped. What was supposed to be SpaceX's side hustle became the main event. It's the kind of pivot that sounds absurd until you see the spreadsheet, and then it makes perfect sense. The company that everyone thinks about as a space outfit is actually becoming a cloud infrastructure player with rockets attached.
