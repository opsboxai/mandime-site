Ocean City, New Jersey had a seagull problem so bad that eating lunch outdoors meant constant vigilance against aerial mugging. The birds had essentially taken over the beaches, swooping down on food with zero regard for personal space or the fact that you'd already paid for that sandwich. It got to the point where the town couldn't ignore it anymore.

So they did what sounds like it came straight out of a revenge fantasy: they hired a falconer to deploy trained birds of prey. Harris hawks and falcons now patrol the beaches, and they don't just scare the gulls away. They kill them. They eat them. It's biological warfare dressed up as wildlife management, and it actually works. The aggressive seagull population has dropped noticeably, and beachgoers can now sit down with food without getting absolutely harassed.

The elegance of it is hard to miss. You can't poison seagulls without poisoning everything else that eats them. You can't relocate them because they just come back. But a predator that's faster, smarter, and hungrier than they are? That's a language gulls understand. A few raptors hunting regularly is enough to break the confidence that made these birds brazen in the first place. They learned to stay away from the beaches where they know danger is waiting.

It's the kind of solution that feels almost old-school in a good way. Rather than some chemical or technological workaround, Ocean City basically said: nature has a way of handling this. We're just going to speed it up. And it turns out that's exactly what beaches needed.
