Satellite photos don't lie. Over the past year, Myanmar's northeast has sprouted a network of massive compounds that shouldn't exist, yet they're multiplying faster than authorities can supposedly shut them down. Matt Burgess dug into the imagery and found something wild: these aren't hidden operations. They're sprawling, visible from space, and growing openly despite the Burmese government's supposed crackdown on the cybercriminal syndicates running them.

What makes this remarkable is the scale. We're talking about dozens of these facilities, some the size of small villages, complete with walls, guard posts, and the kind of infrastructure you'd need to house and control hundreds of people at once. These are the nerve centers of massive romance scams, investment fraud schemes, and sextortion rings that target people globally. The fact that they're not hidden, that they're just expanding out in the open, tells you something crucial about how little the official crackdown actually matters on the ground.

The timing is the real story. These compounds keep materializing even as Myanmar's military government claims it's dismantling them. It suggests either the authorities aren't trying as hard as they say, or they simply don't have the power to stop what's happening in these border regions where scam operations have become a major economic engine. Either way, you're looking at a criminal infrastructure that's evolved past the point where it needs to hide.
