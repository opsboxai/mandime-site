Elon Musk just went live with X Money in the US, and it's basically Venmo meets a vanity metal card with your X handle stamped on it. The platform bundles peer-to-peer payments, a digital wallet, and an actual Visa card you can customize, plus it slots into Apple Wallet so you're not juggling apps like some kind of financial chaos agent.

This is the real test of whether X can actually become the "everything app" Musk has been talking about for years. Payments are the hard part. Venmo works because it's friction-free and culturally embedded. Square's Cash app works because it's simple and trustworthy. X Money has to convince people to move money through a platform built by a guy who owns a site currently imploding with bot activity and verification chaos. That's a trust gap money doesn't solve.

The metal card angle is clever in a very 2024 way. People will absolutely buy a status symbol, especially one that broadcasts their online identity. But the real play here isn't the card. It's whether X can keep users in the app for financial transactions and lock in repeat behavior that way. That's where network effects matter. If your friends and your money both live in one place, leaving gets expensive.

Worth watching whether this gains any actual traction or becomes another ghost feature in X's graveyard of half-finished experiments.
