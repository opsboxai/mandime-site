Ducati just rewrote what a 1,200cc adventure tourer is supposed to be. The 2027 Multistrada V4 S Grand Tour isn't a minor refresh. They've taken their definitive long-distance platform and sharpened almost everything that matters: engine, suspension, electronics, and the actual ergonomics that keep you comfortable when you're eating 400 miles in a day.

The V4 engine gets a bump in output, but the real story is how they've tuned it for the kind of riding this bike is built for. On a Grand Tour, you're not hunting apexes. You're managing fuel stops, managing your body, managing time across continents. Ducati engineered accordingly. The electronics suite expanded too. More refined ride modes, better cornering ABS implementation, adaptive power delivery that doesn't feel like it's babying you but is actually thinking three corners ahead.

What pushes this from competent to compelling is the suspension. They've rethought the setup for real-world touring loads. Two people and luggage isn't a corner case for the Grand Tour, it's the entire business model. The chassis handles that without going soft when you're riding solo. That's harder to engineer than it sounds and most brands don't bother.

At this tier, you're choosing between the Multistrada, the BMW GS, the KTM, and maybe a Triumph. Those are all good bikes. But Ducati built this one knowing exactly who rides it and why. That clarity shows in the details. For a brand with Italian engineering in its DNA, this refresh feels like them doubling down on what they do best instead of chasing everyone else's checklist.
