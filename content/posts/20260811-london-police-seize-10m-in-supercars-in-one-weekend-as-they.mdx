London's Metropolitan Police just pulled off something that would make any car guy's stomach drop. In a single weekend operation, they seized nearly $10 million worth of supercars—Ferraris, Lamborghinis, McLarens—from drivers caught street racing and doing burnouts in the city. We're talking 38 vehicles impounded in 48 hours.

The operation wasn't some random crackdown. It was laser-focused on antisocial driving, which in London means organized street takeovers, illegal drifts, and the kind of show-off driving that gets entire neighborhoods complaining. The police have gotten aggressive about this lately because it's genuinely dangerous. These guys aren't track-testing their six-figure cars on closed courses. They're doing it on public streets where pedestrians, cyclists, and other drivers have zero warning.

What's wild is the scale. Nearly $10 million in vehicles off the street in one weekend suggests this isn't a handful of hot-heads. It's an organized scene, and the police are clearly onto it. The financial hit matters too—impound fees, towing, potential fines on top of the seized cars themselves. For guys dropping half a million on a Ferrari just to thrash it through Mayfair at 2 AM, the consequences are adding up fast. London's basically saying: your car collection doesn't matter if you're using it to terrorize the street.
