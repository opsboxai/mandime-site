The Mac Mini shortage is real, and it's hitting harder than most Apple shortages do. We're not talking about a two-week wait for the color you want. People ordering the base M4 model are looking at six to eight weeks out, and if you want the souped-up version with maxed RAM for AI workloads, you're basically gambling on whether it'll ship before the holidays. Apple's official estimate says November, but supply chains and Apple's estimates have never been close friends.

Here's what's actually happening under the surface. The M4 chip is performing so well for local AI tasks that it's become a cheap way for developers and small businesses to run models without renting cloud compute. That demand alone would've created a crunch, but it's hitting at the exact moment when VRAM and memory modules are already scarce across the industry. Taiwan's semiconductor fabs are running hot, and memory production can't scale as fast as everyone suddenly wants it to. The result is a perfect storm where the most affordable Mac that can actually run serious AI workloads is essentially backordered.

Prices haven't officially gone up, but the market's doing what markets do. Third-party sellers are asking premiums on the secondary market, and Apple's own configuration options are forcing buyers into more expensive builds if they want any buffer in shipping time. If you need a Mac Mini right now and don't want to wait until winter, you're either paying extra or settling for something less than what you actually wanted. That's the real price of the shortage.
