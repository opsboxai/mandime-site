The cars with the highest driver death rates aren't what you'd expect. It's not just the underpowered economy boxes or the elderly-demo sedans. Road & Track pulled data from the NHTSA showing that muscle cars, actual sports cars, and compact vehicles dominate the list of highest fatalities per million registered vehicles. The Chevy Corvette, Dodge Challenger, Ford Mustang, Nissan 370Z, and Hyundai Veloster all crack the top of the chart. These aren't unsafe cars in the traditional sense. They're fast, they're exciting, and they attract drivers who treat them like they should be fast and exciting on public roads.

The inverse is starker. Three-row SUVs basically own the bottom of the list, with some logging zero driver deaths across their entire registered fleet. The Toyota Highlander, Honda Pilot, and Kia Sorento are the safest rides by the numbers. They're heavier, sit higher, offer more metal between you and impact, and more importantly, they don't advertise performance. Nobody buys a Highlander to test its limits at triple-digit speeds on a Tuesday night.

The data isn't actually saying the Corvette is an unsafe car. It's saying the Corvette attracts a driver behavior that kills. A high-horsepower coupe in the hands of someone who bought it specifically to feel that horsepower will always produce different outcomes than a three-row family hauler driven by someone who just wants to get to soccer practice. Physics doesn't care which car you're in when you wrap it around a tree at 110 mph. But it does care a lot whether you're doing 110 in the first place.
