A V-10 is one of those engine configurations that exists almost entirely because engineers wanted to see if they could. Ten cylinders in a naturally aspirated setup doesn't make economic sense, doesn't optimize efficiency, and frankly doesn't fit in most cars without some serious architectural gymnastics. Which is exactly why the best V-10 cars matter. They're proof that when a manufacturer decides pure mechanical theater trumps spreadsheet logic, something special happens.

Road and Track rounded up the standouts, and the list reads like a collection of last stands. The Dodge Viper tops it, which feels right for a car that basically said "what if we just made a truck engine scream like it was being murdered" and called it a supercar. Then you've got the Lexus LFA, a car Toyota spent years perfecting because they could, and the BMW M5 generation that proved a luxury sedan didn't have to apologize for having a naturally aspirated ten-cylinder beating in its chest. The Audi R8 V-10 showed up too, which makes sense for a car that made V-10 ownership feel attainable if you were the right kind of loaded.

What makes this list feel urgent now is that none of these will ever exist again. Turbocharging has won. Electrification is coming. V-10s are extinct. The cars on this list represent a specific moment in automotive history when manufacturers still believed there was value in making something louder, less efficient, and more viscerally alive than what the data suggested was rational. That's the real story here. These aren't just good cars. They're the last ones.
