Toyota just added 0.02 inches to the shift gate on the 2027 GR86's manual transmission. That's it. That's the mechanical update everyone's talking about.

But here's what matters: they're fixing the one thing manual drivers actually complained about. The gap between fourth and fifth gear was tight enough that you'd occasionally catch fifth when you meant fourth, or vice versa. Muscle memory doesn't work when the car won't let you trust your own hands. Toyota listened to that specific frustration and widened the gate just enough to make the shift feel intentional again, not like you're threading a needle at 6,000 rpm.

The rest of the updates are throttle response tuning—refinements to how the engine reacts when you ask it to accelerate. Nothing revolutionary, nothing that shows up in a spec sheet or a YouTube thumbnail. But that's the job of a mid-cycle refresh on a car that already does its job well. The GR86 doesn't need to be reimagined. It needs to be slightly better at being itself, and apparently that starts with letting drivers actually enjoy shifting without second-guessing the transmission.
