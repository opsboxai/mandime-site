Strong glutes aren't just about looking better in jeans. They're load-bearing infrastructure for basically everything else your body does: running, jumping, climbing stairs without grabbing the rail like you're 80, preventing lower back pain that sneaks up at 35, and keeping your knees from turning into landmines. The problem is most guys treat leg day like it's just about quads, and by 40 they're walking like a compass with a bent needle.

Dean Stattmann rounded up what trainers actually consider non-negotiable for glute development, and the lineup skews practical over fancy. You've got your fundamentals (squats, deadlifts, hip thrusts) but the real insight is in the variations. There's a twist on the hip thrust that one trainer calls the ultimate glute exercise, which is the kind of specific claim that means either they've tested it to death or they're selling something. The fact that it made the cut suggests the first one. The routine hits the glutes from multiple angles because that's how you actually build them—not just heavy weight in one plane, but tension and time under load in ways that actually carve definition into the posterior chain.

The smarter move is treating glute work like you'd treat any other muscle group: pick a few movements, get strong in them, and be consistent. Most guys skip this entirely because they think it's vanity work. It's not. It's injury prevention and functional strength masquerading as aesthetics.
