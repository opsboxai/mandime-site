Imagine this: a massive, ancient film projector, the size of a small car, chugging away in a theater, bringing to life the grandeur of Christopher Nolan’s latest epic, The Odyssey. These beasts of burden are the unsung heroes of the film industry, and they’re a dying breed.

IMAX 70mm projectors are the pinnacle of film projection, but they’re also relics of a bygone era. There are only 25 of these projectors in the US, and each one is a behemoth of mechanical engineering. The film itself is 70mm wide, and it runs horizontally through the projector, creating an image that’s incredibly bright and sharp. But these machines are not just about size; they’re about precision. The film is delicate, and the projectors need to be meticulously maintained to avoid scratches and breaks.

Dan Drobik, Technical Manager at IMAX Melbourne, describes the process of reviving a decommissioned projector as akin to putting together a giant Lego set. The projectors are so complex that they require regular use to stay in good condition. Michael Ford, head projectionist at BFI IMAX in London, notes that running the projectors frequently helps catch and address issues before they become major problems.

One of the most common issues is the water cooling system, which keeps the xenon bulb at a safe temperature. Over time, the tubing can wear out, leading to leaks and even bulb explosions. Drobik recounts a particularly harrowing incident where a projectionist had to manually pour water into the system for three hours to keep the show going. “He's sitting there completely soaked, pouring water for three hours straight,” Drobik says. “But the people in the cinema are none the wiser.”

The scarcity of parts is another looming issue. Many of the subcontractors that once supplied parts have closed shop, leaving IMAX with a finite supply of critical components. “IMAX does a fantastic job of keeping these incredibly old systems alive, but at the same time there is this sort of endpoint,” Drobik explains. The projectors are a testament to the enduring appeal of film, but their days are numbered. The next time you catch a flick in IMAX 70mm, remember the silent giants working behind the scenes to bring it all to life.
