Scott Speed just ran the Mount Washington hill climb in 5 minutes and 11 seconds flat. That doesn't sound wild until you learn he beat Travis Pastrana's record by over 16 seconds. On a mountain road. In New England. In a car that was specifically built for this one run.

The weapon was a purpose-built Subaru WRX called Project Midnight, and the math here is worth understanding. Mount Washington's auto road climbs 7.6 miles with a 12 percent average grade and turns tight enough that you're never actually accelerating hard. It's the opposite of a drag strip. Pastrana held the record for years with a time that seemed untouchable, the kind of performance gap that normally requires someone to find a completely different line or a car with another hundred horsepower. Speed erased that by over a quarter minute. That's not incremental. That's a statement.

The real story isn't just that he was fast. It's that Subaru and Speed went to the effort of engineering a specific car for this specific mountain, which means we're looking at suspension geometry tuned for that exact road, braking systems calibrated for those exact turns, and a driver who had one job and prepared for it like it mattered. Because it did. In a sport obsessed with laptimes and records, Mount Washington is hill climb royalty. It's the one people remember. And Speed doesn't just beat Pastrana's mark, he stomps it by 16 seconds. That's a generational performance.
