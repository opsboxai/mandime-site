The honest answer is somewhere between every two weeks and every eight weeks, depending on what you're actually trying to keep. But that range is useless without context, so here's what matters.

If you're running a fade or undercut, you're looking at every two to three weeks minimum. That's not negotiable. The whole point of those cuts is clean lines and visible shape, and hair grows about a quarter inch every week. Wait four weeks and you've got a fade that's more blob than definition. You're basically just letting your barber's work disappear into fuzz.

For longer styles, crew cuts, and textured crops, you can stretch it to four to six weeks. The shape holds longer because you've got actual length to work with, and a little growth actually adds texture and movement. This is where guys usually start coasting and then suddenly realize they look like they haven't seen scissors in two months.

The real variable is your hair type and how fast it grows. Thick, coarse hair shows growth faster and reads as messy quicker. Fine hair can hide extra length longer but loses shape easier. And your routine matters too. If you're getting regular fades and want them crisp, every three weeks becomes the baseline. If you're okay with a slightly softer look and can commit to regular styling, you might stretch it to four.

The trap is treating the haircut like a once-a-season thing and then wondering why you don't look like the guy in the chair at the barbershop. Consistency beats perfection. Find a barber you trust, lock in a schedule that actually works for your life, and stick to it. That's the whole game.
