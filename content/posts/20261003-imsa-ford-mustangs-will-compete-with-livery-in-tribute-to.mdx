A century and a quarter ago, Henry Ford won a single race and then basically never raced again. This weekend, IMSA Mustangs are running a special livery that pays tribute to that one-off 1901 victory, the kind of detail most racing series would bury in a press release footnote. But here's what makes it worth knowing: Ford's racing career lasted about as long as a pit stop, yet that win mattered enough that his company is still commemorating it 125 years later.

The actual history is stranger than the tribute. Ford built a race car called the Sweepstakes, pushed it to a win against Alexander Winton's more famous machine, then walked away from competitive racing to focus on building cars regular people could afford. It's the opposite of every billionaire racing team you see today. He proved he could do it, then decided there were better things to do with his time and money. The Mustang drivers this weekend get to carry that through in a throwback livery that connects modern factory racing back to the moment Ford decided he was done with it.

Whether you care about IMSA or not, there's something clean about a car brand remembering a win so old that the original driver would need a time machine to recognize a modern Mustang. It's not trying to be subtle or clever. It's just a company saying, "This happened, it mattered, and we're still proud enough to paint it on our cars.
