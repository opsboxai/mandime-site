Lamborghini just dropped a limited run of 60 Revueltos, one for each year the Miura has been around, and if you know anything about that car, you understand why this matters. The Miura didn't just exist in 1966—it basically invented the mid-engine supercar template that every exotic manufacturer has copied ever since. Ferrari, Porsche, McLaren: they all followed the blueprint Lamborghini laid down. Now, six decades later, they're marking that anniversary with a special edition of their new hybrid flagship that nods back to what made the original car so revolutionary.

The 60-unit run is the kind of deliberate, restrained move that actually feels rare in a market saturated with numbered editions. Each one gets unique badging and styling details that reference the Miura's proportions and design language, though Lamborghini kept the mechanical specs locked to the regular Revuelto's setup: the 1001-horsepower hybrid V12 and that four-wheel-drive system. The point isn't to build a retro car or a museum piece. It's to acknowledge that without that 1966 gamble, without Marcello Gandini's wild lines and Sant'Agata's willingness to put the engine behind the driver instead of in front, the supercar world looks completely different.

What's interesting is the timing. The Revuelto itself marks Lamborghini's transition away from pure internal combustion, and they're doing it while celebrating a car that was equally transformative in its own moment. The Miura forced the industry to rethink what a sports car could be. The Revuelto is doing something similar now, proving a hybrid V12 can be both relevant and visceral. That's a continuity worth remembering, especially when you're signing a seven-figure check.
