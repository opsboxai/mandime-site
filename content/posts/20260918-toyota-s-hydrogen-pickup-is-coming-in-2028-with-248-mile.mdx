Toyota's actually building a hydrogen pickup truck and it's showing up in 2028 with a 248-mile range. Not a concept. Not a maybe. They confirmed the Hilux hydrogen variant is real and coming, which is genuinely wild because every automaker has basically written hydrogen off for consumer vehicles. Tesla ate everyone's lunch on EVs, the charging infrastructure got too cheap to ignore, and hydrogen just became the thing engineers whispered about in bars.

But here's why this matters: Toyota isn't chasing some fantasy fuel. They've been running hydrogen forklifts and buses in Japan for years. They know the tech works at scale. The Hilux is one of the toughest vehicles on the planet, the thing contractors and fleet operators actually depend on, not something that needs a Tesla Supercharger network to survive. If they can make hydrogen work anywhere, it's in a truck that spends its life hauling cargo in places where electric charging is basically nonexistent. 248 miles is respectable for a work vehicle. That's a full workday, maybe two.

The real question is infrastructure. Hydrogen stations are scattered across maybe five continents, and most of them are concentrated in California, Japan, and parts of Europe. Toyota's betting that hydrogen makes sense in markets where that network actually exists. For everyone else, it's still an interesting technology watching from the sidelines while EVs own the conversation. But if you're running operations in a hydrogen-ready region and need something that doesn't quit, this truck could be the move in five years.
