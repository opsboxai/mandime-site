A genuine geomagnetic storm is hitting this weekend, and if you're anywhere from the northern Great Lakes up through Montana and the Pacific Northwest, you might actually see the aurora borealis without flying to Alaska. The forecast is calling for displays Friday through Sunday, which means if you've got even a halfway clear night and minimal light pollution, this is the window.

Here's the thing most people miss: aurora forecasts are notoriously fickle. The space weather prediction models are way better than they were five years ago, but they're still educated guesses. A G3-level geomagnetic storm (which is what this looks like) can push the lights further south than usual, but clouds will kill it faster than you can drive north. If you're serious about this, check the forecast Friday morning, pick the clearest night in that three-day window, and get yourself somewhere dark. A state park at least 20 miles from the nearest city does the job. Bring a good camera if you have one, but honestly, your eyes will catch way more than your phone ever will.
