A 191-mile thru-hike through the Kamnik-Savinja Alps just opened in Slovenia, and it's almost comically stacked with things most serious hikers chase across continents. Mountain views that snap off like postcards. Trail towns with restaurants serving proper local food and, yes, wine bars that sit literally on the route. Virtually no crowds. Solid infrastructure that doesn't require a guidebook from 2003 to navigate. It's the kind of route that makes you wonder why it took this long to build out.

The Kamnik-Savinja Water Trail runs across Slovenia's northeastern Alps in what's become an increasingly rare setup for European hiking: a region substantial enough to demand serious time and effort, but developed enough that you're not eating protein powder from a ziplock or committing to sketchy weather calls with zero shelter. The trail follows a water system through the mountains, which means constant access to water, fewer mystery route-finding moments, and the kind of natural logic to the path that makes for cleaner hiking. Trail towns are spaced at intervals that feel like someone actually considered what a hiker needs, not just what a logistics planner could squeeze in.

What separates this from the well-worn GR trails that funnel thousands through the same bottlenecks every season is the sheer emptiness of it. You're not hiking shoulder-to-shoulder with people taking selfies at checkpoints. You get the alpine views and the amenities without the audience, which is worth something real if you're actually trying to spend time in mountains instead of just collecting them. For anyone eyeing a serious European hike without the lottery odds of a Camino slot or the crowds of Mont Blanc's standard routes, this one earned its spot on the list the minute it opened.
