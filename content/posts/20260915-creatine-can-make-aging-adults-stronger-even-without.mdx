Here's the wild part: you don't have to earn it. Middle-aged guys taking creatine gained measurable muscle strength even when they did absolutely nothing in the gym. No weights. No cardio. Just the supplement and time.

That's the headline from recent research that upends the standard pitch. Creatine's always been sold as the sidekick to serious training, the thing you take *after* you've already put in work. But this study flipped that script. Adults in their 50s and 60s who supplemented with creatine alone saw meaningful gains in muscle function compared to a placebo group, without touching a barbell. The mechanism is real: creatine fuels your muscles' ATP production, the energy currency cells need to contract and function. It doesn't care whether that demand comes from squats or just existing.

The practical angle here matters because it reframes what aging strength loss actually is. It's not just about "use it or lose it" anymore. It's also about what your muscles have access to at the cellular level. That said, this isn't permission to skip the gym. The real gains happen when training meets supplementation. But if you're 50-plus, beat up, recovering slowly, or just starting to take strength seriously again, creatine at 3 to 5 grams daily is cheap, studied to death, and apparently works whether you're training or not. Most guys waste way more money on worse bets.
