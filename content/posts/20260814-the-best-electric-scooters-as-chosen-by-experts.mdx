Electric scooters have gone from novelty to actual transportation, and the category has matured enough that you can stop thinking about them as toys. The real question now isn't whether to get one, but which one makes sense for how you actually move around.

Road & Track pulled together a solid rundown of the current landscape, and the gap between the cheap stuff and the machines that actually work is wider than most people realize. A $300 scooter and a $1,000 scooter aren't in the same sport. Range matters more than the specs sheet says it does, especially if you're doing last-mile commuting in a city where you can't just call a car. Build quality matters too, because a scooter that wobbles at speed or dies mid-ride stops being convenient real fast and starts being a liability.

The best ones do one thing well: they get you from point A to point B without drama, without killing your knees, and without needing a backup plan when the battery dips. If you're serious about actually using this for commuting rather than weekends, that's the filter worth applying. The cheap wins don't hold up.
