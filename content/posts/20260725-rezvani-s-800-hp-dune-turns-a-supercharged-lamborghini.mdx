Rezvani took a Lamborghini Huracán and stripped it down to something genuinely unhinged. The result is the Dune, and it's got eleven inches of ground clearance, a supercharger screaming 800 horsepower through its engine bay, and only seven build slots available. Ever.

This isn't a casual restomod. They welded new suspension geometry into a carbon fiber chassis, bolted on an air intake that sits high enough to ford shallow water, and wrapped everything in desert-ready bodywork that looks like it spent the last decade being chased across Baja by people who actually know how to drive. The Huracán's 5.2-liter V10 gets a supercharger kit that pushes boost into territory most owners would never dream of exploring, all while maintaining the kind of reliability you'd want when you're hours from the nearest service station.

The eleven inches of ground clearance is the real move here. That's not sedan-level practicality. That's enough to clear obstacles that would destroy a stock supercar without a second thought. They engineered a fully independent suspension with off-road geometry, which means you're not just clearing terrain, you're actually controlling it. The whole package sits on 37-inch tires wrapped around lightweight forged wheels, turning what started as a track machine into something that works in environments where Lamborghinis literally don't belong.

With only seven examples planned, this is the kind of car that becomes a conversation piece for the next twenty years. Not because it's rare, but because it's proof that sometimes the most interesting machines come from looking at what exists and asking, "What if we made it completely stupid in the right way?
