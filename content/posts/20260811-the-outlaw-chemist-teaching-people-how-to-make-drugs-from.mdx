There's a guy on YouTube with over 120 videos documenting the exact chemistry required to synthesize LSD, DMT, psilocybin, and a half-dozen other controlled substances from basic precursor chemicals. His name is Willy Myco. He's not hiding. The videos are public. He breaks down molecular structures, walks through extraction timelines, troubleshoots failed batches, and answers questions in the comments like he's running a legitimate chemistry tutorial channel, because technically he is.

The wild part isn't that someone's doing this. It's that he's doing it well enough that people are actually following along. Mattha Busby's reporting for Wired digs into what happens when an underground chemist decides radical transparency is the answer to drug policy failure. Myco's argument is straightforward: if people are going to make these compounds anyway, they should at least know the chemistry. Bad synthesis leads to contaminated products, overdoses, and deaths. His videos are, in his view, harm reduction at scale.

Not everyone agrees. Law enforcement watches him. Some researchers who study psychedelics think he's reckless. Other chemists in the space think he's burning down the whole operation for everyone else. And there's a legitimate question buried in the drama: is publishing instructions for synthesizing Schedule I drugs actually protected speech, or is it conspiracy? The legal answer isn't settled. The practical answer is that Willy Myco is still posting, his audience is growing, and the conversation about what people should be allowed to know about chemistry is messier than it's ever been.
