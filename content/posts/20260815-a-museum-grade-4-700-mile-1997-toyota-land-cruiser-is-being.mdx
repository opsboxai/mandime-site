A 1997 Toyota Land Cruiser with 4,700 miles is about to convince a lot of people that they've been maintaining their cars all wrong. This isn't a restoration project or a garage find somebody cleaned up. This is a time capsule that spent three decades barely driven, and it's heading to Mecum's Monterey auction in 2026 where it will almost certainly reset expectations for what an old truck can be worth.

The numbers alone are wild. A vehicle from the Clinton administration rolling onto a block with fewer miles than some people put on a car in a single year creates a scarcity that modern production can't touch. The 100-series Land Cruiser is already a legend among people who actually need vehicles to work, but this example exists in a different category. It's not a truck that survived decades of use. It's a truck that barely lived at all.

What matters here is the psychological shift this represents. For years, the collector market treated Toyota's workhorses as tools, not treasures. A well-maintained Land Cruiser meant you took care of something you used. A 4,700-mile example means someone understood they were holding onto something that would matter later. The hammer price will tell the story of how much the market has finally caught up to that logic.
