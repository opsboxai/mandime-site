Spyker just announced its first new car in over a decade, and it's a proper supercar built for someone who actually wants to drive it instead of park it in a climate-controlled vault. The C8 Preliator XXV Coupé is hand-assembled in the Netherlands with a 5.0-liter twin-turbo V10 making 627 horsepower, which puts it in legitimate competition with cars like the Ferrari F8 and Lamborghini Revuelto, except nobody else is making anything quite like it anymore.

The Dutch company nearly went under around 2014, and for years it looked done. But Spyker's back with something genuinely different. The XXV designation references the company's 25th anniversary, and they're only building 25 of them. That's not marketing scarcity, that's just how they operate. The car rides on a carbon fiber and aluminum chassis, tips the scales at around 1,350 kilograms, and comes fitted with a six-speed manual transmission because apparently some car companies still believe drivers should have a say in what their cars do.

What makes this actually interesting isn't just the specs on paper. It's that Spyker never chased market trends the way everyone else did. While Ferrari was spinning up hybrid systems and Mercedes was going full electric, this small Dutch outfit stayed stubborn about building fast, lightweight coupes with soul. The Preliator costs roughly 900,000 euros and hits 330 kilometers per hour. That's not exotic car money compared to hypercars, but it's positioned as a car you can actually own and use, not a spec investment. Whether they can actually move 25 units in today's market is another question, but at least someone's still making the thing enthusiasts keep saying they want.
