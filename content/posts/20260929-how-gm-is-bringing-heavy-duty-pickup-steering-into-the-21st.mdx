GM just killed a steering system that's been industry standard on heavy-duty trucks for over 50 years. Chevy and GMC are swapping recirculating ball steering with hydraulic assist for rack-and-pinion with electric power steering, EPS. That's a bigger deal than it sounds.

Recirculating ball was never elegant. Steel balls roll through grooves in a worm gear, hydraulic pressure does the work, and you get a truck that's mechanically solid but disconnected from the road. It's been the heavy-duty default because it's simple, bulletproof, and handles the torque load without drama. The tradeoff: numb, vague steering and a setup that doesn't play nice with modern driver assist tech that needs actual feedback from the wheels.

Rack-and-pinion with EPS changes the equation. The system is lighter, more responsive, and actually talks back to the driver. More important for GM's play here, it integrates cleanly with the electronic control systems that new trucks need for hands-free highway driving, adaptive cruise, lane centering. You can't bolt autonomous features onto 1970s steering architecture without compromises. This isn't nostalgia territory anymore.

There's a mechanical reality underneath too. EPS doesn't bleed power into keeping the hydraulic system warm and pressurized at idle. On a truck that hauls for eight hours a day, that's real efficiency. The engineering has to handle the same weight and force a heavy-duty chassis demands, but the payoff is a truck that feels modern without sacrificing the capability that buyers actually depend on.
