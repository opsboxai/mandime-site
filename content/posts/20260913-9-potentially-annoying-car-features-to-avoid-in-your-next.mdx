Motortrend pulled together nine car features that sound good on a spreadsheet but turn into daily annoyances once you own the car. The list hits the usual suspects, but the real value isn't what made the cut, it's the why behind each one.

Touch-sensitive controls and capacitive buttons rank high because they don't work when your hands are wet, gloved, or sweaty, which is basically half the year depending on where you live. Manufacturers love them because they're clean and minimal in the cabin. Owners hate them because a physical button works every single time. Same goes for those piano black plastic panels that look premium for about three weeks before they're covered in fingerprints and reflect every light source in the cabin like a mirror ball.

The real killers are the ones that affect core functionality. Touch screen infotainment systems that handle climate control force you to take your eyes off the road for basic adjustments, and they're often laggy or unresponsive compared to the knobs and dials they replaced. Overly complicated menu structures, automatic stop-start engines that shut off at red lights and take a half-second to restart, and adaptive headlights that don't work as well as traditional units also made the list. Then there's the small stuff that compounds, like seat massagers you'll never use, unnecessary driver assistance features that ping at you constantly, and door handles that hide until you approach the car, which means standing there waving your hand like an idiot when the sensor doesn't register you.

The takeaway: just because a feature exists doesn't mean it belongs in your car. Before you buy, sit in the thing and actually use the controls. If something feels gimmicky or gets in the way of what actually matters, it will annoy you every single day for the next five to ten years.
