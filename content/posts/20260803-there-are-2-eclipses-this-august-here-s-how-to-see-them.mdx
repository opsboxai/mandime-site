August 2026 is stacking two eclipses back to back, and if you're into skywatching, this is worth clearing your calendar for. A solar eclipse hits August 12 and a lunar eclipse follows on August 26, which means you get two shots at something most people will never see twice in a lifetime.

The solar eclipse on the 12th is the main event. It's a total eclipse, meaning the moon slides directly in front of the sun and kills the light completely, but the path of totality is narrow and specific. You'll need to be in the right geographic zone to see the full effect, otherwise you get a partial eclipse, which is fine but not the same animal. The lunar eclipse later that month is easier since it's visible from anywhere on Earth where the moon is above the horizon at that moment. That one happens when Earth slides between the sun and moon, casting Earth's shadow on the lunar surface and turning it a deep copper red.

For the solar eclipse, totality is the thing to chase if you can. Standard sunglasses won't cut it. You need ISO 12312-2 certified eclipse glasses or a solar filter on binoculars or telescopes. Most places along the path are selling them now, but grab them early because they disappear fast once people realize the eclipse is actually happening. For the lunar eclipse, no special equipment needed. Just find a clear sky and watch the moon darken over a couple of hours.

If you're already thinking about travel, start planning now. Eclipse chasers are a real demographic, and hotel rooms in the path of totality fill up months in advance. August 2026 isn't tomorrow, but it's close enough that serious skywatchers are already locking down their spots.
