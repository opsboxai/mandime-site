Tom King wrote The Oath, The Vision, and some of the most psychologically fractured Batman stories DC ever published. Then he went to work on Lanterns, HBO's new Green Lantern show, and spent the first season apparently trying to break his own audience the same way he breaks his characters.

King's approach here is exactly what you'd expect from a guy who spent years mining the dark corners of superhero mythology. The show opens with what he describes as a "knock-them-to-the-side" premiere that establishes the stakes immediately. This isn't a slow-burn origin story or a guy getting a magic ring and learning to cope. It's a mystery wrapped in dread, where two Lanterns are thrown into something dangerous enough that the opening episode ends in a place that's supposed to make you sit still for a second after the credits roll.

What's interesting is King's self-awareness about being "the nerd in the room," which feels like an acknowledgment that he knows superhero TV is full of producers trying to sand down the weird parts to make it palatable. He's not doing that. He's bringing the same sensibility that made his runs on Batman land so hard, where the text is sharp and the stakes feel genuinely claustrophobic. In Lanterns, that means a story where you're never quite sure what's going to happen next, and the ending of that first episode is built to make you need answers immediately.

HBO gave him the room to make something that feels like it actually trusts its audience to handle discomfort and complexity. That's rarer than it should be for superhero television.
