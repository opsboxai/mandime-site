The Koenigsegg Agera RS hits 330 mph in a straight line. A Swedish hypercar that costs more than a house and weighs as much as a compact sedan. Then someone built an electric bike that's faster.

The Delfast Top 3.0 has hit 348 mph, which means a two-wheeled electric vehicle just lapped one of the most exclusive supercars ever made. That's the kind of number that doesn't compute at first. You're reading it right. Not close. Not theoretical. Not in some perfect wind tunnel simulation. They actually did it.

Here's where it gets weird though. Top speed is almost a useless metric for anything that's not a salt flat. The Agera RS is engineered to be a genuinely drivable car. You can take it to Monaco, navigate traffic, exist in the real world. The Delfast exists to prove a number exists. It's a narrow-tired, barely-rideable point being made at 348 mph. One is a feat of engineering. One is a flex. Both are ridiculous, but they're ridiculous in completely different ways.

The real story isn't that a bike beat a supercar on the speedometer. It's that electric propulsion has gotten so efficient that a two-wheeled vehicle with a battery and a motor can outrun a $2.6 million hypercar built specifically to be fast. The future's already here. It just doesn't feel like it when you're stuck in traffic.
