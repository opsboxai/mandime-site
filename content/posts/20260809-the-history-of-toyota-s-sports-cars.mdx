Toyota's sports car lineage is basically the opposite of what most people think. You picture Toyota and you get a Camry, a Corolla, maybe a Tacoma. But strip that away and there's an actual throughline of genuinely interesting machines that prove the brand knew how to build something that made you want to drive faster.

The 2000GT was the real beginning, and it still matters. Built in the early 1960s and only produced in tiny numbers, it was the first thing Toyota made that wasn't about getting from point A to point B efficiently. It was about getting there because you wanted to. That car didn't have to exist. But it did, and it showed up in a James Bond film, which meant it mattered globally before most people even knew Toyota made anything fun.

Then came the Celica and the Supra, the models that actually built the reputation. The Celica ran for decades and kept reinventing itself, each generation sharper than the last. The Supra though, especially the A80 from 1993 onward, became the car that people actually modified, raced, and obsessed over. That wasn't accidental. That was Toyota deciding they wanted to be in that conversation with Nissan and Mazda and everyone else building affordable performance.

The 86 and the new GR Supra are the current answer. Not the most powerful, not the fastest in a straight line. But they're built on the idea that driving should feel like something. That matters more than the horsepower number. It's the same philosophy that made the 2000GT worth building and the A80 Supra worth tuning into legend.
