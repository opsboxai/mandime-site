Costco's fuel program is one of the best-kept money moves in America, but there's a catch most members don't know about: diesel is nowhere near as available as regular gas. While Costco operates over 550 fuel stations across the country, only a fraction of them actually pump diesel. The company has never published an official list, which means most diesel-driving members either waste time hunting or give up and pay full price elsewhere.

The real problem is that Costco built most of its fuel infrastructure around high-volume regular gasoline sales. Diesel requires different tank systems, separate pumps, and less predictable demand patterns, especially compared to the steady stream of cars filling up on the way home from work. That's why diesel stations cluster in specific regions where demand actually exists: California, Texas, the Pacific Northwest, and scattered locations in the Midwest and Northeast. If you're driving a truck or diesel sedan in the wrong state, your Costco membership doesn't help you on fuel day.

Your best move is to call ahead. Hit up your local Costco gas station and ask directly if they carry diesel, or check Costco's fuel locator tool on their website and filter by location. Some members have built informal maps in online communities, which actually beats guessing. If you drive diesel regularly, knowing which warehouse near your commute has it isn't just convenient, it's the difference between saving 40 cents a gallon and paying retail.
