Casio just sold out its entire Japan launch inventory of the F-B100W in hours. Not days. Hours. That's the kind of velocity usually reserved for limited sneaker drops, not a digital watch that costs under $100.

The F-B100W is what happens when you take the F-91W, the $25 plastic icon that's been on wrists since 1989, and actually give it what people have been mentally photoshopping onto it for decades: real fitness tracking. We're talking heart rate, step counting, calorie burn, sleep monitoring, stress detection. All the stuff that made smartwatches blow up, but on a form factor that doesn't look like you're wearing a smartphone on your wrist. The design is unmistakably F-91W, right down to the matte plastic and that distinctive LCD face, but thicker and with a sensor array underneath.

Here's what matters: the F-91W became a cult object precisely because it's the opposite of everything that happened to watches. It's not trying to be premium. It's not trying to be smart in the way Apple thinks smart means. It just works, costs nothing, and somehow became a shorthand for "I don't take myself seriously but I also take myself seriously." That paradox is exactly why a fitness tracker wearing that aesthetic resonates hard. Your grandpa wore an F-91W to the beach in 1995. Your college roommate wore one because it was ironic. Your gym buddy would wear one because it actually does what it's supposed to do without the pretense.

The fact that Casio can't keep them in stock isn't hype. It's proof that there's real appetite for watches that remember what watches were supposed to be before the industry decided every wrist needed a tiny black rectangle running an operating system.
