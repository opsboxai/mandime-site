LinkedIn finally did what everyone's been thinking: they built a button to mark posts as AI slop. The Verge's Jay Peters found that the platform is rolling out a reporting option that lets you flag content that looks machine-generated, part of a broader effort to cut down on the endless tide of motivational gibberish and recycled corporate wisdom flooding the feed.

Here's what makes this actually interesting. LinkedIn isn't just adding another generic report button. They're explicitly acknowledging that AI-generated content has become such a visible problem that users need a dedicated tool to surface it. The button sits alongside existing report options like spam or harassment, which means LinkedIn is treating algorithmic slop the same way it treats actual bad behavior. That's a meaningful shift for a platform that's spent years letting its algorithm reward exactly this kind of content: the low-effort, high-engagement posts that sound like they were written by someone who read 100 LinkedIn motivational posts and fed them into a blender.

The timing matters too. LinkedIn's feed has become almost unreadable if you're there for actual professional content. For every genuine industry insight or job opportunity, there are dozens of posts that read like they were generated by someone who trained an AI model on LinkedIn's worst posts from 2019. The platform can blame users all day, but they've built an algorithm that incentivizes this exact stuff. Adding a report button is damage control, but at least it's acknowledging the damage exists. Whether it actually improves the feed depends entirely on whether LinkedIn does anything with the reports when they come in.
