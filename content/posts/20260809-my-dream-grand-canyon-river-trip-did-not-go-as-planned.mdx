Sierra Shafer's parents met on a river trip, which means her dream of running the Colorado through the Grand Canyon wasn't some casual bucket list item. It was inheritance. The kind of thing you carry for decades, the way some guys carry a fishing rod their grandfather touched or a route their old man climbed. You imagine it perfectly. You tell the story of it to yourself a thousand times. Then you actually go.

What makes this worth reading isn't that things went wrong. Things always go wrong on river trips. What matters is what Shafer figured out when the reality didn't match the dream. The Grand Canyon doesn't care what you expected. The river doesn't negotiate. You can spend your whole life building up a moment in your head, and then you're actually in it, and it's completely different from what you rehearsed, and that gap between imagination and reality is where most people either break or finally learn something true. That's where the actual story lives. Not in the whitewater or the scenery or the moment you finally checked the box. In what you learn about yourself when the box refuses to be checked the way you planned.
