David Cifaldi was about ten miles from the nearest road when his trekking pole punched through his ribs. He'd fallen on granite near Granite Peak in Montana, and the steel tip went straight in, missing his lungs by what doctors would later call luck. A helicopter rescue would cost him north of $15,000 out of pocket. So instead of waiting for help, he started walking out.

What makes this story remarkable isn't just that he did it, but how methodically he documented it. Mile by mile through rough backcountry terrain with a steel shaft embedded in his torso, Cifaldi kept moving, kept aware of his body's signals, and kept his head. He knew stopping meant bleeding out faster. He knew panic meant mistakes. He knew the distance to help mattered more than the pain. The mile-by-mile breakdown Outside Online pulled together isn't a hiking triumph narrative dressed up for clicks. It's a survival account of a guy doing the brutal, specific math of staying alive when the easy choice would have killed him.

The detail that stays with you isn't the injury itself. It's that he made it out, that the math worked, and that sometimes the difference between a survival story and a tragedy is ten miles of bad terrain and the decision to keep moving.
