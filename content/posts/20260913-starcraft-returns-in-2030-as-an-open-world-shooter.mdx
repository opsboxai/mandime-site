Blizzard is finally making StarCraft: Ghost, just 22 years too late. The studio announced a new open-world StarCraft shooter coming in 2030, which means they're essentially giving the franchise a hard reset after the original Ghost got axed in 2014 following more than a decade in development hell. That first attempt started in 2002. Let that sink in.

The original Ghost was supposed to be a 3D tactical shooter following a ghost operative through the StarCraft universe, a natural bridge between the real-time strategy that defined the franchise and something you could actually play in first person. It never happened. Instead, Blizzard watched competitors build entire empires on that exact formula. Halo did it. Destiny did it. Even Call of Duty found ways to fold sci-fi into its playbook. Meanwhile, StarCraft stayed locked in the strategy lane, incredibly successful there but invisible to anyone who didn't care about base building and unit micromanagement.

The new version, just called StarCraft with no Ghost branding, is being built from scratch with an open-world structure, which means Blizzard isn't just dusting off old designs. They're building something that fits where shooters actually live in 2030, not where they were in 2002. The question isn't whether they can pull it off technically. It's whether StarCraft as a universe still matters to anyone outside the esports circuit. Six years is a long time to build momentum for anything. Longer when you're asking people to care about a property that's been radio silent on the consumer side since the last proper single-player campaign in 2015.
