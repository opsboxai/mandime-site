Tom Place was 18 when he bought the Rolex Submariner. He was also 18 when he lost it, diving in a lake that same year, watching it slip off his wrist and vanish into the murk. That was 1993. He's been thinking about it ever since.

Three decades is a long time to carry around a ghost story about a watch. Most people would've moved on, bought another one, framed the loss as a lesson learned young. Place isn't most people. He's a stuntman, which means he makes his living doing things other people won't. Thirty years later, he decided he was going back into that lake.

What makes this worth actually caring about isn't the watch itself, though a Submariner from that era carries real weight. It's the stubborn refusal to let something go. A Rolex isn't irreplaceable—there are thousands of them. But this one is his, sitting on the bottom of a lake like a tiny time capsule, untouched since the Clinton administration. The question isn't whether he'll find it. The question is what it means that he had to try. Austin Rogers followed him back out there to find out, and what happened next is the kind of story that only works because someone actually cared enough to show up and document a man chasing something he lost before most people had even heard of the internet.
