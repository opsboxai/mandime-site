The US and Mexico just announced they're running a joint operation against cartel drones, and they're using laser technology to do it. This isn't theoretical stuff either. They're actually deploying systems that can detect, track, and disable commercial drones that the cartels have weaponized for trafficking runs and surveillance.

Here's what makes this real: cartels have been flying drones across the border for years now, moving product and staying invisible to traditional radar. Commercial drones are cheap, untraceable, and disposable. The technology to shoot them down with lasers exists, but getting two countries to actually coordinate on it is the harder part. The fact that they announced this publicly means they're confident enough in the system to advertise it, which also serves as a deterrent.

The laser angle is crucial because it's precision work. You're not lobbing missiles at the border. You're identifying a specific drone, locking onto it, and neutralizing it without collateral damage. That matters when you're operating in populated areas and across an international line. Whether this actually disrupts the trafficking networks or just becomes another cost of doing business for the cartels remains to be seen, but at least someone's finally treating drone interdiction like an actual problem instead of a future worry.
