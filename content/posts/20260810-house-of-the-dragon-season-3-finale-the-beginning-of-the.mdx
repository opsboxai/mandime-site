The House of the Dragon finale just torched any remaining hope that this war ends with a negotiated peace. William Goodman's piece lays out what went down: a kingdom so thoroughly fractured that even when dragons stop burning things, the actual bleeding probably doesn't stop. That's the thing about civil wars in Westeros—they don't have clean endings, and the show isn't pretending they do.

What makes this finale work is that it doesn't resolve anything. It just makes everything worse in ways that feel inevitable rather than shocking. The power structures that kept the realm together for centuries are gone. The dragons are dying. The families that held the throne are turning on each other not because of honor or ambition anymore, but out of pure survival. By the time season four rolls around—the final season—there won't be much left to salvage. That's the entire setup. Not a dramatic climax but a slow erosion of everything.

The brutality here isn't gratuitous either. It's the logical endpoint of decisions made earlier in the season when both sides still thought they could win. Now they're fighting because stopping means death. That's the kind of corner characters paint themselves into that keeps people watching, and the show's leaving itself exactly where it needs to be to end it all in one final season.
