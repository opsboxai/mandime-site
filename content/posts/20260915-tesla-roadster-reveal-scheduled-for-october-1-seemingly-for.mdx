Tesla's been dangling the Roadster reveal for so long that announcing a date almost feels like a prank at this point. But October 1 is locked in, and this time there's actually something visual to hang it on: the teaser image shows what looks like thruster ports on the rear, which means Elon's not just talking about the wild specs anymore—they're apparently committed enough to actually show what the thing looks like.

The rocket thrusters aren't new talk. Tesla's been mentioning cold gas propulsion as a legit feature since Roadster 2.0 was first announced, supposedly to hit 0-60 in under three seconds and top out north of 250 mph. It sounds like nonsense until you remember that SpaceX literally owns Tesla, and cold gas thrusters are actual rocket tech that absolutely could give a car a physics cheat code. Whether it'll actually work as advertised or remain the automotive equivalent of vaporware is another question entirely, but the fact that they're teasing the hardware in marketing materials suggests they're at least taking the bit seriously.

The real story here is that this reveal actually has to happen. The original Roadster launched in 2008 and got discontinued in 2012. The second generation was announced in 2017 and promised for 2020. We're now deep enough into the 2020s that "late 2024" for a reveal feels borderline reasonable, which tells you how far behind schedule this thing is. If October 1 actually delivers something real and not just another delay announcement, it'll be the first actual confirmation that this car exists outside of Elon's Twitter feed.
