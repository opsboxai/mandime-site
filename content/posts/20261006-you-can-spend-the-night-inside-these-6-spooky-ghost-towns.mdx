Ghost towns aren't supposed to be comfortable, which is exactly why you should spend a night in one. Kathleen Rellihan rounded up six actual places where you can sleep in restored miners' cabins, desert outposts, and genuine abandoned structures across Montana, Nevada, and California. These aren't theme park recreations or boutique hotels playing dress-up. They're real towns people left behind, and they still feel it.

The appeal cuts deeper than Instagram aesthetics. You get the isolation without the complete disconnection. Enough infrastructure to sleep and eat, but enough emptiness that you actually feel the weight of why these places emptied out in the first place. A cabin in a Montana mining town tells a different story than a tent at a managed campground. The difference is the silence, the absence, the sense that you're staying in something that was built for a purpose that's now gone.

The logistics matter too. These aren't five-hour drives to nowhere. They're accessible enough that you can actually get there and back without losing a weekend to driving, but far enough off the usual tourist circuit that they haven't been sanitized or overcrowded. It's the kind of trip that feels like discovery, even though it's documented and bookable. That's the real sell here. Not the spook factor, but the genuine strangeness of being somewhere that time essentially abandoned.
