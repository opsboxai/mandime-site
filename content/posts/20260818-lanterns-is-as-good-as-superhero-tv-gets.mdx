HBO's Lanterns pulled off something superhero TV has struggled with for a decade: it made the cape stuff feel like the B-plot. The show opens as a murder mystery in a small Louisiana town, complete with noir atmosphere and real detective work. The Green Lantern Corps exists in this world, sure, but it's treated like the mechanism underneath, not the spectacle on top. That's the inversion that works.

The trick is that Lanterns respects the detective story first. Two cops with actual history and friction investigate a body that doesn't add up. They follow leads. They clash over methods. They uncover something bigger. It's the structure of True Detective or Mare of Easttown, which means the pacing works for people who've never read a comic and don't care about ring mythology. The superhero elements land harder when they're earned through real narrative tension instead of grafted onto a generic origin blueprint.

What makes this stand out in the post-Marvel TV graveyard is the refusal to wink at the camera or treat the source material like a checklist to appease. Lanterns commits to being a serious show that happens to have superpowers in it, not a superhero show that's trying to look serious. That distinction has killed dozens of attempts. This one gets it right, which means people who want actual storytelling will watch it, and people who want the DC universe will too. That's the rare overlap.
