A Swedish research team just proved what every driver already suspected: modern car touchscreens are catastrophically worse than buttons at keeping your eyes on the road. They ran an industry-first test comparing how long drivers look away from the road when using a touchscreen versus a physical control, and the numbers are brutal. Touchscreen interactions pull your attention away for significantly longer stretches, and more dangerously, they keep you glancing back and forth as you hunt for what you're doing, whereas a button press is one glance and you're done.

The real problem is precision. Your fingers can't feel a touchscreen. With a knob or button, muscle memory handles 80 percent of the work. Your hand knows where it is. With a screen, you have to look, aim, tap, then look again to confirm it worked. If it didn't register, you look again. If the menu changed, you look again. A simple climate adjustment becomes a three or four-second distraction instead of a one-second one. At highway speeds, that's 300 feet of road you're not watching.

Car manufacturers know this. They've known it for years. But touchscreens look premium, they're cheaper to manufacture at scale than custom button layouts, and they let automakers push over-the-air updates to change how controls work months after you bought the car. So they keep shipping vehicles with screens as the primary interface for functions that used to have dedicated hardware. This test won't change that. It might, however, make the next lawsuit settlement slightly larger.
