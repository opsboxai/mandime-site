There's something uniquely depressing about a car that does everything right except make you feel anything. It gets you there. It starts. The warranty covers it. And yet every time you turn the key, you're piloting what amounts to a beige spreadsheet with wheels.

Readers across the internet weighed in on which cars have perfected the art of automotive apathy, and the results paint a pretty clear picture: Toyota, Chevrolet, Stellantis, and Tesla keep showing up in the list of offenders. These are companies that have either engineered the soul out of their vehicles or never bothered putting one in to begin with. The Camry. The Cruze. The Dodge Charger electric. The Model 3. Cars that tick boxes but don't stick with you. They're competent. They're forgettable. They're everything a car shouldn't be if you actually care about driving.

What's interesting is that "soulless" isn't really about horsepower or tech specs. It's about intention. It's that a car feels like it was designed by committee to hit a price point and a regulatory requirement, not because someone actually wanted to build something worth wanting. It's the difference between engineering and creation. And right now, the industry is drowning in the former while starving the latter.
