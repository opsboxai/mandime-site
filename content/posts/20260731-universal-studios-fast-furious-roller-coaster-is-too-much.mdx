Universal Studios just opened a new Fast & Furious roller coaster, and it's apparently so fun that neighbors are suing over it. The ride, which opened in spring 2024, is built to mimic high-speed street racing with actual engine sounds, tire squeals, and the kind of adrenaline-fueled screaming you'd expect from a coaster that hits 60 mph. The problem: Toluca Lake residents live close enough to hear all of it, all day, and they're done.

This isn't the first time a theme park has faced noise backlash, but the Fast & Furious coaster hits different because it's designed to be loud. The whole point is sensory overload. Riders are supposed to feel and hear the chaos. And they do, so well that people living a quarter mile away say they can't enjoy their own yards or open their windows without hearing a constant loop of strangers screaming. Some residents report the noise starts early and runs late, turning what was once a quiet neighborhood into an extension of the park's sound design.

Universal's response has been measured. They've done acoustic testing, installed some noise barriers, and pointed out that the park was there first. Legally, they probably win that argument. But the real tension here is about what happens when entertainment and residential life occupy the same square mile. The coaster generates revenue and jobs. The screaming generates real disruption. Both things are true at once, which is why these disputes tend to drag on. Toluca Lake residents aren't wrong about the noise. Universal isn't wrong about operating a functioning attraction. Someone's just going to have to lose sleep over it until a settlement shows up.
