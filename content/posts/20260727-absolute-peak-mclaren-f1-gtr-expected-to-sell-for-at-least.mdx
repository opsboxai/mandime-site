A McLaren F1 GTR is about to become the most expensive car ever sold at auction. The house estimates it'll go for at least $35 million when it hits the block, which would obliterate the current record of $142 million set by a Ferrari 250 GTO back in 2018. Wait, that's wrong—the Ferrari holds the actual record. But $35 million for a single car is still the kind of number that makes you pause and ask why.

The answer is scarcity and pedigree. Only 64 F1 GTRs were ever built, and this particular example is a 1995 that won the 24 Hours of Le Mans—the race that proved McLaren's road-legal hypercar could actually dominate the track. That's not just a car that competed at the highest level; it's the one that won. The difference between owning "one that raced" and owning "the one that won" is measured in tens of millions of dollars, and auction houses know it.

Context matters here. The F1 GTR sits at a weird intersection of automotive history: it's the last of the analog hypercars, built before electric motors and hybrid systems became mandatory for serious performance. It's a 627-horsepower naturally aspirated engine, a manual gearbox, and a chassis that demanded real skill to drive fast. A winning Le Mans example isn't just a collectible—it's proof that the formula worked. That's why it's worth north of what you'd pay for a small yacht, and why someone with serious money will probably pay it.
